## Key Question 1: What would each major option cost per month and per transaction for a single-location pizza restaurant and bar (dine-in, carryout, beer-and-wine-to-go)?

### Takeaway
List prices cluster at $0-$69/month for entry software (Square, Toast, TouchBistro, Lightspeed Starter, SkyTab) and $99-$245/month for pizza-specialist or reseller-sold systems (Thrive, HungerRush, Revel, Clover via ISO), but processing dominates total cost: at roughly $80,000/month of in-person card volume, the spread between a 1.99%-2.3% negotiated rate and a 2.75%-3.09% flat rate is about $400-$800/month, which is 5-10x the software subscription. First-party online ordering costs 3.5%-6% of order value on flat-fee platforms versus 15%-30% on marketplaces.

Worked example (ESTIMATE, computed by the researcher from the list rates cited above; assumes a single-location pizza-and-bar with $80,000/month in-person card volume across 2,300 card transactions, average ticket about $35, two terminals; plus 500 first-party online orders/month at $40 = $20,000 CNP volume). Monthly in-person cost = software + rate x volume + per-item fee x count:
- Square Plus: $49 + (2.6% x 80,000 = $2,080) + (2,300 x $0.15 = $345) = about $2,474/month; effective 3.09% incl. software. Square free tier: about $2,425.

- Clover direct Table Service $89.95 at 2.3% + 10 cents: $90 + $1,840 + $230 = about $2,160 (2.70%). Via a reseller at $135-$245/month and 2.3%-3.5%: roughly $2,200-$3,275.
- SpotOn Essentials, 2 stations ($110) at 2.45% + 15 cents: $110 + $1,960 + $345 = about $2,415 (3.02%); at NerdWallet's 1.99% + 25 cents: $110 + $1,592 + $575 = about $2,277 (2.85%).
- SkyTab, 2 terminals ($60) + annual program fee amortized ($500/12 = $42) at 2.75% + 15 cents: $102 + $2,200 + $345 = about $2,647 (3.31%); at a negotiated 2.3% + 10 cents: about $2,172 (2.72%).
- Interchange-plus benchmark (any BYO-processor system): modeled effective rates of about 2.0%-2.1% for a mixed in-person card mix (see KQ7) imply about $1,600-$1,680/month in processing on $80,000, i.e. $500-$1,100/month less than the flat-rate options above before software.

- ChowNow Pro: $229 + 2.95% ($590) + 500 x $0.29 ($145) = about $964 (4.8%).
- BentoBox: $49 + 500 x $0.99 ($495) + 3% ($600) = about $1,144 (5.7%).

### Inferences
- For this restaurant profile, the subscription line is a rounding error next to processing; a 0.5-point processing difference on $80,000/month ($400) is worth more than the entire gap between Square's free tier and Lightspeed Essentials. Vendors that give away software or hardware (Toast Starter Kit at $0, SkyTab free hardware at $29.99) recover it in a 2.75%-3.09% blended rate.
- The pizza-specialist systems (Thrive, HungerRush, SpeedLine) cost 2-3x the entry price of the generalists in software, and at least Thrive also binds the operator to its own processing, so their pitch has to be delivery/driver management and pizza modifiers rather than price.
- Flat-fee first-party ordering lands at 3.5%-6.2% of order value (plus any guest-side fee), i.e. one-third to one-fifth of marketplace commissions; the flat-monthly products (Owner.com $499, Popmenu, ChowNow Premier) only beat the per-transaction products (Square Online, BentoBox) above roughly 400-600 orders/month.

## Key Question 2: Which vendors let a restaurant bring its own processor, which force their own payments, and how do processing rates compare?

### Takeaway
The dominant 2025-2026 pattern is "own payments required": Toast (all tiers), Square, SkyTab/Shift4, Thrive and Clover (via the selling bank/ISO) all tie software to their processing; a processor-side blog lists SpotOn, Revel, Lightspeed and TouchBistro as allowing outside processors, but that claim is from an interested party and conflicts with reports that those vendors now default new accounts to their own payments, so it needs verification.

### Cited Findings
- Toast requires Toast Payments on every standard tier (Starter Kit, Point of Sale, Build Your Own); "there is no 'bring your own processor' option on Toast's published plans" — [deelo.ai Toast pricing 2026](https://www.deelo.ai/blog/toast-pos-pricing-2026); [sleftpayments Toast vs independent processing](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/toast-pos-pricing-vs-independent-processing-2026) (both are competing-processor content)
- Toast hardware only works with Toast; "if you switch POS systems, the hardware becomes a paperweight" — [sleftpayments Toast problems 2026](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/toast-pos-problems-complaints-2026)
- Thrive: "every plan also requires ThrivePay processing" — [Thrive pricing via search](https://www.thrivepos.com/pricing); [posusa Thrive review](https://www.posusa.com/thrive-pos-review/)
- SkyTab: processing through Shift4 required; standard 2.75% + $0.15, reported negotiated ranges 2.4%-2.75%, and one claim of "as low as 0.04% + $0.04" for high volume (implausible as a total rate; probably a markup-over-interchange quote) — [merchantcostconsulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/skytab-pos-review/); [top10.com SkyTab review](https://www.top10.com/pos/reviews/skytab); [posusa Shift4 Dine review](https://www.posusa.com/shift4-dine-pos-review/)
- Clover: sold through banks, ISOs and processors; "every Wells Fargo, Bank of America, and independent agent who resells Clover sets their own monthly fees and processing rates"; dual-pricing or interchange-plus deals are available from some resellers — [azrestaurantpartners.com](https://azrestaurantpartners.com/toast-vs-clover.html); [Katalyst three-way comparison](https://www.katalystos.com/reviews/toast-vs-square-vs-clover); [paymentsinfull Substack](https://paymentsinfull.substack.com/p/clover-vs-square-vs-toast)
- A processor's blog lists "SpotOn, Revel Systems, Lightspeed Restaurant, and TouchBistro" as POS systems that allow outside processors (marketing content; "needs checking against each vendor's current terms") — [sleftpayments why restaurants leaving Toast](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/why-restaurants-leaving-toast-pos-2026)
- Rate comparison from review sites: Square 2.6% + 15 cents in-person; Toast 2.49% + 15 cents (hardware purchased) or 3.09% + 15 cents (pay-as-you-go); SpotOn 1.99% + 25 cents on paid plans (NerdWallet) or 2.45% + 15 cents / 2.79% + 20 cents (tech.co); Clover 2.3% + 10 cents direct, 2.3%-3.5% + 10 cents via resellers; SkyTab 2.75% + 15 cents list — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/best/small-business/restaurant-pos-systems); [tech.co](https://tech.co/pos-system/best-restaurant-pos-systems); [nextdoorpayments](https://nextdoorpayments.com/clover/for-restaurants); [merchantcostconsulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/skytab-pos-review/)
- Toast management attributes 2025 take-rate gains to "cost optimization, small pricing moves, and new products such as surcharging" — [Yahoo Finance Toast Q4 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-inc-tost-q4-2025-050116070.html); [Investing.com Toast Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings-call-transcript-toast-q3-2025-misses-eps-but-beats-on-revenue-93CH-4332448)
- Reddit (quoted second-hand by a processor blog, unverified): an r/restaurantowners poster asked Toast to lower its rate and "was told it was take-it-or-leave-it," noting the whole operation runs on Toast hardware — [sleftpayments Toast problems](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/toast-pos-problems-complaints-2026)

### Inferences
- The only places a small operator can realistically shop processing are Clover (by playing resellers against each other) and whichever of SpotOn/Lightspeed/TouchBistro/Revel genuinely still accept third-party gateways; everything else is a bundled take-it-or-leave-it rate. Any new entrant that offers interchange-plus with a transparent markup and does not lock hardware to the gateway is differentiated on the single largest line item in the operator's tech bill.
- Clover's reseller-set pricing means a Michigan bank's or ISO's quote may be cheaper or far more expensive than "Clover's price"; the brand is not a price.

### Gaps
- No primary confirmation (vendor terms pages) of which systems allow third-party processors in 2025-2026; the one list found is from a competing processor. In particular, Lightspeed and TouchBistro reportedly require their own payments for new US accounts, but no source captured this run confirms either way.
- HungerRush, SpeedLine, Heartland and Aloha (NCR Voyix) processor requirements were not captured.
- No published Toast rate card exists; the 2.49%/3.09% figures are review-site snapshots of unknown date.

## Key Question 3: Which platforms expose an API a small developer can use without a partner contract, and which gate it?

### Takeaway
Square and Clover have self-serve developer sandboxes and public documentation (Clover additionally reviews production accounts and App Market listings); Toast publishes docs but issues credentials only through its partner program or to a restaurant group's own developers; SpotOn requires a sales rep or CSM to enable API keys; Olo is the most closed, with a login-gated portal, tiered partner program and no self-serve sandbox. Aloha was not captured this run.

### Cited Findings
- Toast: developer docs are public, but "credentials are issued only after a vendor is accepted into the partner program"; one source describes an eight-stage partner process ending in certification with a sandbox build and signed agreement; fees are unpublished, the only figure cited being a 2019 report of a 30% revenue share (likely outdated) — [Katalyst: Toast's partner API fees aren't published anywhere](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/pos-api-access-and-what-integrations-cost); [directorders.com Toast Partner API guide](https://www.directorders.com/blog/toast-partner-api-restaurant-guide)
- Toast account types: Partner API accounts for integrators working across restaurants outside a single management group; "restaurant management group API accounts" for developers working on one restaurant group's locations — [Toast developer guide: API accounts](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiClientAccounts.html)
- Toast API overview lists access tiers per endpoint: "Partner integrations," "Custom integrations," and "Standard API access" — [Toast API overview](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiOverview.html); one review gives a rate limit of 20 requests/second — [directorders.com](https://www.directorders.com/blog/toast-partner-api-restaurant-guide)
- Square: "open API for custom work"; marketplace of 100+ self-serve apps across business types; documented Terminal API letting developers connect Square Terminal to their own POS/ERP (2020 announcement) — [Katalyst Toast vs Square](https://www.katalystos.com/reviews/toast-vs-square); [NerdWallet Toast vs Square](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/toast-vs-square); [BusinessWire Square Terminal API 2020](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201015005168/en)
- Clover: sandbox environment available before any approval, with a test payment gateway; production access requires a separate per-region developer account that Clover's team reviews and approves; apps then pass functional, legal/privacy and market-listing reviews before publication to the Clover App Market; North American accounts now sit on a "global developer platform" — [Clover docs: sandbox](https://docs.clover.com/docs/get-started-with-sandbox-environment); [Clover docs: app approval](https://docs.clover.com/docs/clover-app-approval-process); [Clover docs: sandbox to launch checklist](https://docs.clover.com/docs/from-sandbox-to-app-launch-checklist); [Clover global developer platform quick steps](https://docs.clover.com/dev/docs/global-developer-platform-quick-steps)
- SpotOn: enterprise developer center page says it would be deprecated 12/31/23 and points to venuenext.dev; "API keys are not self-provisioned. Access requires contacting a sales rep or customer success manager, who then enables specific venues"; a third-party catalog lists a Restaurant POS Export API keyed per location — [SpotOn enterprise docs](https://developers.spoton.com/enterprise/docs); [SpotOn enabling API access](https://developers.spoton.com/enterprise/docs/enabling-api-access); [apis.io SpotOn](https://apis.io/providers/spoton/)
- Olo: partner program tiers Developer, Silver, Gold, Platinum — [Olo Connect partner program guide (PDF)](https://partners.olo.com/downloads/olo-connect-partner-program-guide.pdf); a third-party API report card says the developer portal is login-gated with no public API reference and no self-serve sandbox, and that partners must apply through Olo Connect and be sponsored by a brand (sponsorship requirement unverified against Olo's own guide) — [supergood.ai Olo API report card](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/olo); project-based API access granted per brand via Olo support — [Fishbowl Olo docs](https://docs.fishbowl.com/knowledge/olo)
- Olo scale: 750+ restaurant brands, about 88,000 locations, 400+ integration partners; taken private by Thoma Bravo at $10.25/share (about $2.0 billion), announced July 2025, expected to close by end of 2025 — [NRN Olo Thoma Bravo](https://www.nrn.com/mergers-acquisitions/olo-to-be-acquired-by-software-investment-firm-thoma-bravo); [Goodwin announcement](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/news/2025/07/announcements-technology-ma-olo-to-be-taken-private); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/thoma-bravo-agrees-buy-olo-094353597.html)

### Inferences
- A small studio can build and test against Square and Clover today with no contract; shipping to Clover's App Market or Square's marketplace adds a review step but not a sales relationship. Toast's "restaurant management group" account is a loophole for a bespoke build for one owner's locations, but not for a product sold to many Toast restaurants, which requires the partner program.
- Olo is effectively irrelevant to a single independent pizzeria both as a customer (enterprise brands) and as an integration target (brand-sponsored partner access).

### Gaps

## Key Question 4: What do operators say they want and cannot get (flat fees, data ownership, honest support, self-serve menu editing, reliable offline, a kitchen screen that works)?

### Takeaway

## Key Question 5: How do independents make the decision (local rep, peer recommendation, price, feature) and how long do they keep a system?

### Takeaway
Channel shapes the decision: Toast sells direct, Clover arrives through the operator's bank or an ISO agent, and the pizza specialists sell through reps and dealers; surveys show high stated switching intent (44% plan to replace/upgrade in 2026; 53% say they bought or switched in the past year, down from 71%) driven by features (60%) and cost (57%), while inertia is explained by time cost (37%), 2-3 year contracts with auto-renewal, and $3,000-$6,000+ per-location migration cost.

## Key Question 7: What are current card-present and card-not-present processing economics (interchange-plus vs flat rate) that a new platform would be built on?

### Takeaway
Visa's published consumer-credit interchange runs about 1.51%-2.30% + 10 cents card-present and 1.89%-2.60% + 10 cents card-not-present, so modeled all-in interchange-plus effective rates sit near 2.0%-2.1% in-person and 2.5% online versus 2.7%-2.95% in-person and 3.2% online on flat-rate pricing; Toast's reported net payments take is 48-49 basis points of volume, which is the margin a bundled POS keeps over its own costs and the yardstick for any new entrant's payments revenue.

### Cited Findings
- Visa US interchange (schedule effective April 18, 2026, as cited by a processor): consumer credit card-present from 1.51% + $0.10 (All Other Products) to 2.30% + $0.10 (Visa Infinite spend-qualified); card-not-present credit Product 1 from 1.89% + $0.10 to 2.60% + $0.10 — [Celer Merchants flat rate vs interchange-plus](https://celermerchants.com/guides/flat-rate-vs-interchange-plus/)
- Visa Traditional Rewards: 1.65% + $0.10 in person vs 2.04% + $0.10 card-not-present — [Clearo Payments](https://clearopayments.com/blog/interchange-plus-vs-flat-rate/)
- Modeled effective rates: 50% debit / 30% consumer credit / 20% rewards mix about 2.0% on interchange-plus vs about 2.7% flat; with 30% debit / 40% rewards / 30% consumer the gap nearly disappears (about 2.6% vs 2.7%); flat rate can win in a commercial-card-heavy mix — [ProTech Payments](https://protechpayments.com/interchange-plus-vs-flat-rate-pricing/)
- $200,000/month store: 2.95% effective on flat rate vs 2.10% on interchange-plus; ecommerce about 2.55% effective on IC+ vs 3.2% on flat rate — [Merchant Cost Consulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/interchange-plus-savings/)
- Illustrative $80,000 example with a 0.20% + $0.10 IC+ markup: 1.60% effective vs 2.60% flat (markup-only, best case) — [Relyon](https://getrelyon.com/blog/interchange-plus-vs-flat-rate)
- General references on interchange-plus mechanics and average US processing costs — [Payment Depot](https://paymentdepot.com/blog/interchange-plus-pricing/); [Motley Fool average processing fees](https://www.fool.com/money/research/average-credit-card-processing-fees-costs-america/); [Finix](https://finix.com/resources/blogs/interchange-plus-vs-flat-rate)
- Toast take rates: Q3 2025 fintech net take rate 61 bps and payments net take 49 bps; Q4 2025 fintech net take rate 58 bps, payments take rate 48 bps (up 2 bps YoY), attributed to cost optimization, small pricing moves and surcharging; payments ARR growth 24% in Q4; SaaS gross margin 80%; SaaS net retention 109% for 2025 — [Toast Q3 2025 prepared remarks (PDF)](https://s28.q4cdn.com/141746709/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Q3-25-Prepared-Remarks.pdf); [Investing.com Q3 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-toast-q3-2025-misses-eps-but-beats-on-revenue-93CH-4332448); [Yahoo Finance Q4 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-inc-tost-q4-2025-050116070.html); [Globe and Mail Q4 transcript](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/motley/196222/toast-tost-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Toast 2025 revenue mix: fintech solutions $5,037 million vs subscription $936 million; combined GAAP subscription + fintech gross profit $1,818 million ($1,887 million non-GAAP) — [Toast FY2025 annual report (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650164/000165016426000097/toastinc10k2026v1-final.pdf)
- Toast Q1 2026 guidance: fintech + subscription gross profit growth 22%-24% YoY — [Yahoo Finance Q4 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-inc-tost-q4-2025-050116070.html)
- Third-party marketplaces also layer processing: Uber charges a 2.5% + $0.29 order processing fee on top of commission — [getsauce Uber Eats fees](https://www.getsauce.com/post/uber-eats-fees-for-restaurants-2)

### Inferences
- "Software is the loss leader, payments is the product" is visible in Toast's own numbers: fintech revenue is about 5.4x subscription revenue, and management explicitly names surcharging and "small pricing moves" as take-rate levers; the $0 Starter Kit and SkyTab's free hardware are the customer-facing side of the same model. (The split of gross profit between subscription and fintech was not captured; see Gaps.)
- At a 48-49 bps net payments take on roughly $1M of annual card volume for a single pizzeria, Toast's payments margin from that one location is on the order of $5,000/year (estimate), versus $828/year from a $69/month subscription; this is the revenue a new entrant forgoes if it offers pass-through interchange-plus, and the revenue it must replace with software fees or a modest markup (e.g. 20-30 bps).
- Card-not-present interchange is roughly 35-40 bps higher than card-present on the same card, so first-party online ordering is structurally more expensive to process than the dine-in check regardless of platform; the 2.9%-2.95% + 29-30 cents rates charged by Square Online and ChowNow sit about 40-50 bps above a modeled IC+ ecommerce cost.

### Gaps
- Regulated debit (Durbin) interchange and Mastercard schedules were not captured; the modeled mixes above are vendor illustrations, not audited averages.
- No authoritative 2025 industry-average effective rate for restaurants was found (searches returned only processor and consultant blogs).
- Subscription vs fintech gross-profit split, SaaS ARPU and Q4 2025 net location adds for Toast were not captured (SEC exhibit and prepared-remarks PDF could not be fetched; figures above come from secondary summaries that "disagree slightly").
- Michigan surcharging/dual-pricing rules, and embedded-payments (payfac/Stripe Connect/Adyen for Platforms) revenue-share economics for a small ISV, were planned but not searched before the budget ran out.
