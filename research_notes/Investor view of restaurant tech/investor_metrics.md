# Investor view of restaurant tech: how VCs and acquirers evaluate vertical SaaS with embedded payments (2024-2026)

Method note: web fetching of primary pages was blocked by the session proxy (403 on a16z.com, stripe.com, bvp.com, sec.gov, carta.com, substack). All findings below come from search-result extracts of those pages and from secondary write-ups. Where a figure came only from a secondary summary, or from a single source, it is marked. Public-company figures are from SEC exhibits or company press releases unless marked "secondary". Figures are US-market, 2024-2026.

## Key question 1: What do the leading vertical SaaS investors say they look for, in their own published words (source and date)?

### Takeaway
The investor thesis has been stable since 2019-2020 and is restated in 2025-2026 benchmark material: a vertical SaaS company wins a "control point" (the system of record and workflow for a trade), then monetizes that position with embedded payments and other fintech, which investors say lifts revenue per customer 2-5x and makes the business multi-product and stickier. In 2024-2026 the same investors (Bessemer especially) have shifted their public language toward "vertical AI," and the benchmark publishers (Tidemark, Stripe) now treat payments attach, multi-product share and fintech-cohort retention as the headline metrics.

### Cited Findings
- a16z, "Fintech Scales Vertical SaaS" (Aug 4, 2020; authors Kristina Shen, Kimberly Tan, Seema Amble, Angela Strange): "by adding fintech, SaaS businesses can increase revenue per customer by 2-5x and open up new SaaS markets"; the wave "occurs first in vertical markets"; about 90% of public SaaS and the 2019 Forbes Cloud 100 run subscription models — [a16z (English)](https://a16z.com/fintech-scales-vertical-saas/); authorship and date confirmed on [a16z Spanish edition](https://a16z.com/es/fintech-escala-saas-vertical/)
- a16z's core "winner-take-most" claim: the vertical SaaS business that best serves a specific industry often becomes the dominant solution and can then sell financial products to that base; early vertical SaaS resold payments, now they embed loans, cards, insurance — [a16z](https://a16z.com/fintech-scales-vertical-saas/)
- a16z distinguishes two delivery models: a referral model that resells a processor's service vs an embedded model where the software company acts as payment facilitator, giving better payment economics; embedding "works best at scale, usually above $50M of gross merchandise value" (the threshold for justifying setup cost) — [a16z follow-up, Aug 2020](https://a16z.com/2020/08/04/fintech-scales-vertical-saas-2/) (figure as extracted by search summary; single source)

### Inferences
- The investor framework is three-step: (1) own the control point (the register/order flow is the control point in a restaurant), (2) attach payments and expand products, (3) extend to the customers' customers (diners) and suppliers. A restaurant register plus online ordering sits at step 1 with step 2 (payments) and step 3 (diner-facing ordering) already in the product, which maps well onto the thesis.
- Bessemer's 2024-2026 public language has moved to "vertical AI"; a 2026 pitch for a restaurant platform will be evaluated partly on whether AI changes the labor economics of the restaurant, not only on software plus payments.
- Lightspeed Venture Partners and First Round published nothing vertical-SaaS-specific that surfaced; their positions are inferred only from their portfolio (not sourced here).

### Gaps
- Bessemer's "State of the Cloud 2025/2026" and its vertical SaaS index/laws were not retrievable; only 2024-edition summaries surfaced.

- No primary statements from Lightspeed Venture Partners, First Round, or payments-focused funds (e.g., QED, Ribbit) on restaurant tech specifically were found.
- The Point Nine 2025 napkin itself was not retrieved; only a secondary summary.

## Key question 2: What are the current benchmark numbers (ARR split, GPV and take rate, gross margins, NRR/churn, CAC payback, LTV/CAC, locations and revenue per location, attach rates, sales efficiency, Rule of 40), and where do Toast, Olo, Square, SpotOn (and Clover, Lightspeed, Shift4) sit?

### Takeaway
For vertical SMB SaaS the published medians are roughly: ARR growth ~36% (2024 Tidemark median), GRR ~88-91% with fintech-led cohorts at ~96%, NRR ~102-108%, payments attach ~40% average and rising, CAC payback ~9-18 months depending on ACV, software gross margin ~80%. Toast is the reference comp: ~164K locations, $195B GPV, >$2.0B ARR (defined as subscription revenue plus fintech gross profit), net fintech take rate ~58 bps (payments ~48 bps), SaaS gross margin ~80%, NRR ~109%, and a blended gross margin of only ~27% because payments and hardware are low-margin.

### Inferences
- Revenue per location is the unit investors anchor on: Toast ARR ~$2.05B / ~164K locations is roughly $12.5K ARR per location (my arithmetic), of which the majority is fintech gross profit; Olo's ARPU of ~$955/quarter is ~$3.8K/year because Olo sells modules, not the register or payments. A register-plus-ordering startup will be benchmarked against the Toast number.
- Toast's "total monetization" of ~98 bps of GPV is the single most useful target metric: it says that a restaurant platform at scale earns ~1% of what flows through the register as recurring gross profit, about half from payments net take, a tenth from capital, and the rest from software.
- For an early-stage company, investors will expect SMB-style retention: annual logo churn in the 20-30% range is normal for restaurants because of closures, so NRR >100% has to come from location expansion and module attach, exactly as Toast reports.
- SpotOn is the closest private comp (field-sales restaurant POS with embedded payments) but has published nothing usable since 2022.

## Key question 3: How do investors value payments revenue versus software revenue, and what multiples apply in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Investors value payments on gross profit (net revenue after interchange and network fees), not gross processing revenue, and the public comps are built that way: Toast's ARR counts fintech gross profit, Shift4 reports "gross revenue less network fees," and multiples.vc shows Toast at ~2.3x EV/revenue but ~27% blended gross margin. Software revenue carries a higher multiple (public vertical SaaS ~5-8x EV/revenue in late 2025 depending on the index) while embedded payments add value through retention and a 33-60% valuation premium claimed for fintech-embedded vertical SaaS (single advisory source).

### Cited Findings
- Toast's ARR "consists of subscription revenue and fintech gross profits annualized," and LTM gross profit ~$1.8B on ~$6.8B revenue (~27%); EV/Revenue ~2.3x — [multiples.vc, Toast](https://multiples.vc/public-comps/toast-valuation-multiples)
- Shift4 reports "gross revenue less network fees" (+49% in Q1 2026) as its revenue-quality metric; spread is "the average yield Shift4 earns on the average end-to-end payment volume ... after network fees" — [MarketBeat Q4 2025 call](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/shift4-payments-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-02-28/); [Shift4 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794669/000179466925000007/ex991q4.htm)
- Toast and Shift4 both trade at roughly 26x forward earnings per a Q1 2026 comparison; another analysis puts Toast at ~22x earnings with EPS growing ~29%/yr — [HeyGoTrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/blog/toast-tost-vs-shift4-four-b2b-fintech-2026/); [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/it/blog/perche-il-titolo-toast-viene-scambiato-a-22x-gli-utili) (both secondary)
- Vertical vs horizontal SaaS multiples, late 2025: Windsor Drake Q4 2025 report, premium vertical SaaS median 7.8x EV/Revenue vs public horizontal SaaS 5.3x (46% premium); SaaSRise 2026 report, vertical SaaS buyout multiples 5.8x revenue vs 4.1x horizontal (41% premium) — [Windsor Drake](https://windsordrake.com/?p=32566); [SaaSRise Vertical SaaS M&A and VC Report 2026](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/vertical-saas-ma-vc-report-2026) (both advisory sources)
- One advisory report says vertical SaaS companies with embedded fintech commanded 33-60% valuation premiums over software-only peers; another says leading vertical SaaS companies generate 30-40% of revenue from payments, lending and financial services — [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/vertical-saas-ma-vc-report-2026) and [Windsor Drake](https://windsordrake.com/?p=32566) (single-source each, unaudited)
- Broad software multiples compressed: median EV/TTM revenue 3.6x in Q1 2026, down from 6.7x in 2024 and 5.9x in 2025 — [multiples.vc insights](https://multiples.vc/insights/software-saas-valuation-multiples)
- Gross margin drives the multiple: SEG Q2 2025 cohorts, >80% gross margin at 7.2x median EV/TTM revenue vs 3.5x below 60% — [Orb compilation of SEG data](https://www.withorb.com/blog/saas-growth-margin-statistics) (secondary)
- Software Equity Group (via Payabli): embedded payments are an "immediate jump in revenue and gross profit" on an existing customer base rather than a gradual margin change — [Payabli, embedded payments and valuation](https://payabli.com/how-embedded-payments-increase-vertical-saas-valuation/) (vendor)
- Olo take-private: Thoma Bravo paid ~$2.0B equity value ($10.25/share, 65% premium to Apr 30, 2025 close) for a company with $285M 2024 revenue (+25%), ~89K locations, 114% NRR and ~51% gross margin; my arithmetic: ~7.0x trailing 2024 revenue, ~6.2x the Q1 2025 run rate — [Olo 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1431695/000143169525000022/oloq22025exhibit991.htm); [InsideArbitrage](https://www.insidearbitrage.com/2025/07/thoma-bravo-to-acquire-olo-for-2-billion-in-all-cash-deal/); [SaaStr commentary](https://www.saastr.com/pe-loves-saas-again-thoma-bravo-buys-olo-for-2-billion/)
- Worldpay's PE/VC insight paper frames embedded payments explicitly as a "revenue multiplier" for software platforms — [Worldpay, Mar 2025 (PDF)](https://go2platforms.worldpay.com/hubfs/Partnerships/PE-Insights/Mar-2025-Embedded-Payments-as-Revenue-Multiplier.pdf) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Practical valuation rule the comps imply: software ARR at a SaaS multiple (roughly 5-8x for vertical software in late 2025, lower for sub-scale private companies), plus payments at a multiple on net revenue/gross profit (the Toast and Shift4 convention), never on gross processing revenue. A founder who reports gross processing revenue as "revenue" will be re-cut to net by any diligence team.
- Payments' value to an investor is partly indirect: the 96% GRR of fintech-led cohorts (Tidemark) and 11% lower churn (Stripe) raise the multiple on the software ARR as well as adding the payments gross profit.
- Olo at ~6-7x revenue with 114% NRR and 51% gross margin is the current reference for what a strategic/PE buyer pays for scaled restaurant software with ordering and payments (Olo Pay).

### Gaps
- No source gave side-by-side EV/gross-profit multiples for Toast or Shift4 against pure SaaS peers for 2025.
- No source explicitly stated a private-market multiple for payments gross profit vs software ARR at seed/Series A.

## Key question 4: At what point does a bootstrapped studio with paying restaurants become fundable, and what do investors expect to see (stage definitions in dollars, ARR/locations/team, data room)?

### Takeaway
Across Carta, Point Nine and SVB-type data for 2025: pre-seed is a sub-$500K SAFE at a ~$10-15M cap with little or no revenue; seed is a ~$2.5-4M round at ~$16-24M post with $0-1M ARR (half of seed companies are pre-revenue); Series A in 2024-2025 clusters at ~$3M median ARR for SaaS (top quartile ~$7M) with 2-3x YoY growth, though Point Nine's 2025 napkin cites a lower $0.5-2.5M range. No restaurant-tech-specific stage data exists; a solo-founder studio is penalized most at seed and the accepted mitigation is paying customers and visible acquisition velocity before the first priced round.

### Cited Findings
Stage definitions (general SaaS, 2024-2025)
- Carta (Peter Walker, July 2025): SaaS Series A median ARR ~$3M in 2024, 75th percentile nearly $7M; ARR at Series A roughly doubled vs 2021 while valuations rose only modestly; "a revenue multiple is still rather shaky at Series A"; AI startups skew the figures — [Carta, $7M ARR Series A](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-7m-arr-series-a-saas-2025/)
- Carta (July 2025) on seed founders raising an A: VCs "are expecting more revenue, better metrics, and stronger momentum than they ever have" — [Carta, Series A hard market](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-series-a-metrics-saas-hard-market/)
- Carta (Sept 2025): median Series A pre-money $57.5M for AI vs $41.2M non-AI, ~20% dilution in both — [Carta, Series A AI vs non-AI](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-series-a-benchmarks-ai-vs-non-ai-2025/)
- Carta seed data: median pre-money for new seed rounds $16M in Q3 2025 (+14% YoY), median post-money reached $24M in Q4 2025; median 2025 seed round ~$4.0M (FutureSight summary) vs $2.5-3.2M attributed to Carta by a SaaS guide (conflict) — [Carta State of Private Markets Q1 2025](https://carta.com/uk/en/data/state-of-private-markets-q1-2025/); [SaaStr on Carta seed data](https://www.saastr.com/the-state-of-seed-today-10-key-learnings-from-cartas-latest-data); [Metal.so seed benchmarks](https://www.metal.so/collections/us-saas-seed-round-benchmarks-2025-average-round-size-valuations-dilution)
- Pre-seed: rounds under $500K were 97% likely to be pre-seed in 2025 and 93% of those were SAFEs; median post-money SAFE caps ~$10M for $250K-$1M rounds and ~$15M for $1-2.5M rounds — [Kruze on pre-seed (citing Carta)](https://kruzeconsulting.com/preseed-funding/)
- Point Nine 2025 SaaS napkin (95 rounds Jan-Jul 2025, secondary summary): seed ARR $0-1M, seed pre-money $5-15M, round $1-4M; Series A ARR $0.5-2.5M with 2-3x YoY ARR growth; ~half of seed-funded companies still pre-revenue — [Nuoptima summary](https://nuoptima.com/saas-requirements-benchmark-raising-capital). Point Nine's 2023 napkin put Series A at ~$1-5M ARR; a PitchLens summary says "commonly ~$2.5-3M+ ARR" — [Point Nine 2023 napkin](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/what-does-it-take-to-raise-capital-in-saas-in-2023-56d8f617714); [PitchLens](https://www.pitch-lens.com/vc/point-nine-capital)
- Burn multiple: David Sacks' rule of thumb (2x reasonable early-stage, 3x+ a warning) was cited in results but without a retrievable page; see gaps.

Solo founder / key-person
- CRV: investors weigh the founding team heavily at priced seed rounds; the solo-founder gap "narrows by Series A, when business performance drives the decision"; "reaching meaningful traction before the first priced round largely removes the solo-founder penalty"; CRV also cites a peer-reviewed study (3,526 startups) finding solo founders more likely to keep ventures running — [CRV, solo founder](https://www.crv.com/content/solo-founder)
- Eqvista (vendor data, unverified): solo founders ~20% of VC-funded deals vs 37% two-founder teams; investors treat a lone founder as "a single point of failure in all areas" — [Eqvista](https://eqvista.com/solo-founder-fundraising/)
- Sramana Mitra: build to paying customers and "a clear path to high-velocity acquisition"; "if those metrics are in place, you can raise money without a co-founder" — [Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/?p=128316)

Data room
- Law-firm seed checklist (Klehr, 2025): certificate of incorporation and amendments, bylaws/operating agreement, stock ledger and fully diluted cap table including SAFEs and notes, board and shareholder consents — [Klehr Harrison](https://klehr.com/publications/startup-evolution-a-year-long-journey-through-a-founders-toolkit-due-diligence-ready-building-a-data-room-that-wins-investor-confidence/)

- SetSquared (accelerator): formal data rooms are more common in later rounds; seed investors value organized, transparent founders over perfect documents — [SetSquared](https://innovation.setsquared.co.uk/news/2665479)

Restaurant-tech rounds as stage reference points (2025-2026)
- Owner.com: $120M led by Meritech and Headline at $1B valuation (May 2025), "quintuple its value a year ago"; a later aggregator item reports $240M at $2.3B led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives after crossing $100M ARR (unverified) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-13/restaurant-tech-startup-owner-com-hits-1-billion-valuation); [Digg aggregator](https://digg.com/tech/1215vmji)
- Chowbus: $81M led by Prysm and Left Lane (Mar 2026), >$120M ARR, pivoted from delivery app to full-stack restaurant POS — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/chowbus-81-million-raised-for-ai-powered-restaurant-platform/amp/)
- Restaurant365: $135M led by KKR and L Catterton at >$1B (back-office, 2023) — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant365-raises-135m/650748/)
- Early rounds found in directories only: FineDine $1.0M (Jan 2025), Kitchen Stadium parent $1.8M (Feb 2025) — [VC Backed directory](https://www.vcbacked.co/directory/industries/restaurants?page=14) (unverified)

### Inferences
- Mapping the general-SaaS stage bars onto restaurant unit economics (Toast ~$12.5K ARR/location, my arithmetic; a startup without capital products will be lower, say $5-8K): a $1M ARR seed-stage proof point is roughly 100-200 paying locations with payments attached; a $3M ARR Series A is roughly 400-600 locations. These are inferences, not sourced benchmarks.
- For a one-person studio, the fundability threshold the sources describe is "paying customers plus a repeatable acquisition motion" before a priced seed; given Toast's field-sales density argument, a credible local-market density story (one metro with visible share) is the restaurant-specific version of that.
- A seed data room for this company should include, beyond the legal basics: location-level cohort retention (logo and GPV), payments attach and net take rate in bps, software vs payments gross margin split, processor/payfac agreement, PCI scope, and hardware economics; none of the generic checklists list these, but they follow from the metrics investors ask of Toast.

### Gaps

## Key question 5: What are the known red flags that kill restaurant-tech deals, and the known accelerants (moats)?

### Takeaway
Red flags in investor and diligence sources: sub-100% NRR or SMB churn above ~3-5% monthly, gross margin below ~70% on the software line (services or hardware dressed as product), revenue concentrated or discounted, solo-founder dependency at seed, reliance on a single processor (which even Toast lists as a risk), and reporting gross processing volume as revenue. Accelerants: payments attach and the resulting retention lift, multi-product expansion, local market density with a field channel, and data that enables capital products.

### Cited Findings
Red flags
- SMB churn: "high churn is an existential challenge for SMB SaaS. Almost none of the SaaS playbook really works if your NRR is less than 100%"; Lemkin says the only startups he lost money on "had high churn in the early days" — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-challenge-with-smb-saas-high-growth-can-only-mask-high-churn-for-just-so-long/)
- One investor's pass thresholds: monthly churn above 2% ("22%+ of customers annually"), LTV:CAC below 3x, CAC payback beyond 24 months — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/middle-red-flags-when-to-pass-on-a-saas-investment) (one author's rules of thumb)
- Gross margin: below 70% "might raise concerns for investors and analysts, suggesting potential issues with pricing, cost management, or competitive positioning"; hybrid models with professional services have lower margins — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-at/resources/more/saas-gross-margin-explained-what-it-is-and-why-it-is-important); SEG's operations team calls under 70% "a red flag for potential investors" — [Orb compilation](https://www.withorb.com/blog/saas-growth-margin-statistics)
- Services dressed as product: Tom Tunguz, most professional services businesses run at zero or negative gross margin, and when services are 50%+ of revenue they "substantially depress overall gross margin" — [Tom Tunguz](https://www.tomtunguz.com/gross-margin-cashflow-margin-trends-in-saas/); diligence question list: "Are customers renewing because the product is embedded in their workflow? Is revenue spread across many customers, or concentrated in a few accounts? ... Are discounts masking weak willingness to pay?" — [BigIdeasDB diligence checklist](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-acquisition-due-diligence-checklist)
- Key-person: a lone founder is "a single point of failure in all areas"; key-man risk is anyone who "can cause serious harm by issuing an ultimatum," with customers loyal to the person rather than the business — [Eqvista](https://eqvista.com/solo-founder-fundraising/); [SearchFunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/winning-factors-and-warning-beacons-for-smb)
- Single-processor dependence: Toast's own 10-K says it relies "on a limited number of payment processors," that replacing them would be difficult, and that processor-linked outages that stopped customers collecting payments "have occurred before, and similar events may recur" — [Toast FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650164/000165016426000057/tost-20251231.htm)
- Toast's SMB base is flagged as costly to retain and exposed to economic swings; Toast "deliberately targets customers that are costlier to acquire and churn more often," which weighs on retention — [Autobound 10-K summary](https://www.autobound.ai/companies/toast/10-k) (secondary); [Every, Napkin Math on Toast](https://every.to/napkin-math/toast-a-vision-of-software-s-future)
- Per-location volume slippage as a bear case: Toast GPV per location -1% YoY in Q2 and Q4 2025 — [Toast Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650164/000165016425000288/tost-20250630xexhibit991.htm); investor opinion calling the moat "weak" on that basis — [Seeking Alpha comments](https://seekingalpha.com/symbol/TOST/comments) (opinion)
- Price competition: Bessemer's Eric Kaplan, platforms that compete on price end up "in a race to the bottom" — [Adyen report](https://www.adyen.com/campaigns/uk/embedded-payments-report)
- Fintech regulatory risk: FDIC consent orders against BaaS partner banks in Feb 2024 with more through 2024-2025 — [Beancount guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/11/embedded-finance-banking-as-a-service-smb-software-vertical-saas-payments-lending-issued-cards-guide) (secondary)
- Restaurant failure rates cited in analyses of Toast: ~30% of restaurants not surviving year one (student analysis, not primary) — [Harvard D^3 case on Toast](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/toast-inc-the-restaurant-pos-and-management-platform/)

Accelerants / moat
- Payments as lock-in and retention: fintech-led vertical SaaS GRR 96% vs ~90% (Tidemark 2025); embedded financial products cut annual churn 11% and multiproduct platforms grow 49% faster (Stripe 2026) — [SaaSletter](https://www.saasletter.com/p/vertical-saas-benchmarks-2025); [Stripe Sessions 2026 blog](https://stripe.com/blog/vertical-saas-insights-sessions-2026)
- Control point with "data, workflow, and account gravity" gives "the unfair right to sell multiple products" (Yuan) — [Tidemark podcast](https://theplatformjourney.tidemarkcap.com/episodes/25-dave-yuan-tidemark)
- Data to capital: Toast used card-processing data to underwrite working-capital loans; Toast Capital now adds ~10 bps of take rate on GPV — [Apideck](https://apideck.com/blog/embedded-finance-vertical-saas); [MarketBeat Q4 2025 call](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/toast-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-02-13/)
- Sequencing: payments first (every SMB already takes cards), then lending, then insurance/banking/payroll/cards, each layer relying on the previous layer's data — [Conor Witt, Vertical Operating Systems and Embedded Fintech](https://conorwitt.substack.com/p/vertical-operating-systems-and-embedded); [Pipe embedded capital playbook](https://pipe.com/playbook/embedded-capital-playbook-for-vertical-saas)
- Field channel and density: 75-80% of Toast's sales force is field-based in geographic patches; restaurants are dense enough that in-person selling is efficient and "social proof matters more in that density"; Toast still gains share in metros above 30% penetration — [SaaStr CRO Confidential](https://www.saastr.com/cro-confidential-a-look-inside-saas-success-toast-with-cro-jonathan-vassil); [Finviz/Zacks](https://finviz.com/news/145434/australia-launch-amex-deal-will-tosts-recipe-fuel-its-growth-engine)
- Payfac economics as an accelerant: moving from referral (0-20 bps) to managed PayFac (40-100 bps) multiplies payments revenue 5-10x on the same volume — [Payrix 101](https://get.payrix.com/hubfs/Whitepapers/Payrix_Payment_Facilitation_101.pdf); [Payabli](https://payabli.com/category/blog/)
- Recent investor appetite signals: Owner.com's valuation quintupled in a year to $1B (May 2025); Chowbus raised $81M at >$120M ARR (Mar 2026) after pivoting to full-stack POS; Thoma Bravo paid a 65% premium for Olo (Jul 2025) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-13/restaurant-tech-startup-owner-com-hits-1-billion-valuation); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/chowbus-81-million-raised-for-ai-powered-restaurant-platform/amp/); [Olo 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1431695/000143169525000022/oloq22025exhibit991.htm)

### Inferences
- For a register-and-ordering startup built from an existing codebase, the "services business dressed as product" test will be applied to onboarding, menu build and hardware installs: if those are billed as revenue and carried at low margin, the blended gross margin falls under the 70% line that diligence teams flag. Investors will want software subscription separated from implementation and hardware, and payments shown on a net basis.
- Dependence on a single processor is tolerated at scale (Toast discloses it) but at seed the question will be whether the payments relationship is a referral deal (0-20 bps, thin and replaceable) or an embedded/PayFac arrangement with the startup as merchant of record for its sub-merchants, because that determines whether payments revenue is "owned."
- Restaurant closure-driven churn is structural (25-45% logo churn per one vendor); investors compare GPV retention and NRR rather than raw logo churn, so cohort tables by location are the required format.

### Gaps
- No source listed restaurant-tech deals that were killed for specific reasons; red flags come from general SaaS/SMB diligence writing plus Toast's own risk factors.
- No primary VC statement specifically on "custom build vs product" in restaurant tech; closest is general gross-margin and services-mix guidance.
- The Lightspeed Venture Partners, First Round and payments-fund (QED, Ribbit, Infinicept-adjacent) positions on red flags were not found.
