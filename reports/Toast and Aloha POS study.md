# Toast and Aloha Win on Depth, Lose on Trust

Toast and NCR Aloha dominate US restaurant point of sale for opposite reasons, and both leave the same openings for a small challenger. Toast wins new independents with a field sales machine, a $0 or $69 a month entry price and a tightly integrated register, handheld, kitchen screen and online ordering loop; it reached about **180,000 locations in Q2 2026** and earns roughly half of its recurring gross profit from mandatory payment processing at a **48 basis point net take**. Aloha holds its base through on-premises resilience, deep table-service and pizza configuration and local dealers, while NCR Voyix's own earnings calls describe **SMB restaurant revenue falling** and independents leaving for cloud systems. The documented complaints about each are structural: bundled processing with mid-contract rate increases, multi-year auto-renewing contracts with early termination fees, proprietary hardware and slow post-sale support, plus, in Toast's case, the **July 2023 $0.99 guest fee** that was withdrawn nine days after going nationwide once operators and a House committee objected. Around them, Square, Clover, SpotOn and the pizza specialists price software at $0 to $245 a month and recover their margin in a processing spread worth **five to ten times the subscription**. A register for a small Michigan pizza tavern must handle fractional toppings, one-tap pre-authorized bar tabs, Michigan's 6 percent prepared-food rules, the 2026 tipped-wage schedule and sealed-container beer to go. Glazed Web can realistically build that on Stripe Terminal or Square Terminal in two phases of roughly four to six months each, and its flat 99 cent customer-paid order fee, the same number Toast tried, is viable only if the operator chooses it, the guest sees it itemized and the restaurant can absorb it instead.

## Search excerpts rather than full pages underpin every figure here

Every one of the six research notes behind this report opens with the same limitation: the research sandbox blocked direct page fetches to every outside host tried, including Toast's marketing and developer sites, NCR Voyix, SEC EDGAR, Capterra, Trustpilot, the BBB, Reddit, michigan.gov and the Stripe and Square developer docs. All findings therefore come from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages rather than full reads, and quotations are near-verbatim as they appeared in excerpts, unchecked against the page. **No first-hand Reddit thread was obtained** in any note despite repeated targeted queries; every Reddit quote below arrives through a competitor's blog without a link, thread title or date, and is labeled that way. Many of the 2025 and 2026 "review" sources are blogs run by competing processors, POS vendors or lead-generation sites (Katalyst, Sleft Payments, Loman, Owner.com, Chowly, Smart Payment Solutions, Celer Merchants and RapidCents among them), and their figures are treated here as claims with a commercial motive. Where a number appears in only one source it is marked single-source; where sources disagree the range is given and the disagreement is named. Three of the research runs also exhausted their search budget before planned queries on Heartland, Lightspeed and TouchBistro processing rules, Slice's current fee schedule, the status of Michigan's alcohol-to-go sunset and local-first offline toolkits, so those gaps are stated where they matter. Official vendor support pages are the most reliable material here on how products actually behave, third-party aggregators are the most reliable on sentiment, and nothing below should be treated as a verified contract term or rate card without reading the live page.

## Toast sells a loop operators like and a contract they resent

### Payments pay for the free hardware

Toast ended Q2 2026 with about 180,000 locations, up 22 percent year over year, after a record 9,500 net new locations in the quarter, $60.7 billion in quarterly gross payment volume and $2.4 billion in ARR ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3645137/toast-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)). For full-year 2025 it reported **164,000 locations, a record 30,000 net adds, $6.15 billion in revenue and about $195 billion in GPV** ([SEC 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650164/000165016426000057/tost-20251231.htm)). GPV per location was flat to down 1 percent, so essentially all growth comes from adding restaurants, with existing ones ringing no more than before ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-q4-earnings-call-highlights-101233713.html)). The revenue mix explains the pricing: 2025 fintech revenue was **$5.04 billion against $936 million of subscription revenue**, and management reported a payments take rate of 48 basis points and total recurring gross profit of 98 basis points of GPV ([Toast FY2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650164/000165016426000097/toastinc10k2026v1-final.pdf); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-q4-earnings-call-highlights-101233713.html)). A $1.2 million a year independent is therefore worth roughly $12,000 a year in gross profit to Toast, most of it from cards, which is why a terminal can be free and software can be $0 to $69. On share, a January 2026 Baird survey relayed secondhand puts US small-restaurant card processing at Clover about 20 percent, Toast about 17 percent and Square about 13 percent, while a UBS analysis citing IbisWorld puts total US restaurant payments volume at Oracle Micros 30 percent, NCR 16 percent, Toast 16 percent and Clover 8 percent ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/toast-clover-battle-for-small-eateries/809108/); [Built In Dublin](https://builtindublin.ie/company/toast/faq/stability-growth)). Neither underlying report was retrievable, so both share figures are secondhand.

### What reviewers and operators praise

The praise is consistent across G2, Capterra and the trade roundups: restaurant-specific workflow (coursing, seat splits, modifiers, tabs), low training time, the handheld, integrated reporting, offline continuity and 24/7 support availability at every tier. G2 reviewers comparing the two platforms "found Toast easier to use, set up, and administer" than NCR Aloha ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/ncr-aloha-pos-vs-toasttab-com)), and a five-location Capterra reviewer credited offline payments when their ISP failed ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=2)). The hardware is a genuine differentiator: the 2025 Toast Go 3 handheld is IP65-rated, survives five-foot drops and runs more than 24 hours on a charge ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250731400794/en)), and the KDS tracks prep time per station, supports expo coordination and is rated for 120°F kitchens ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/restaurant-pos/kitchen-display-system)). Bar tab pre-authorization opens a named tab with a single swipe and tokenizes the card, though the support page notes dip and tap pre-auth does not yet work on dual-reader setups ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Card-Pre-Authorization-Configuration-Device-Setup)). Menu modeling lets modifier groups be attached at menu-group level and inherited, reused and nested, which cuts the number of modifiers a pizza menu needs ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Adding-Modifier-Groups-and-Modifiers-in-the-Menu-Builder)). Toast Tables undercuts reservations incumbents at **$50 a month for unlimited waitlist and 25 reservations, or $199 unlimited**, with no per-cover fees, against OpenTable at $149 to $499 ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-adds-reservation-waitlist-management-product/648443/)). The consumer app, now Toast Local, charges guests no commission ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Local-by-Toast-FAQ)).

Reviewers segment the fit sharply. NerdWallet's verdict is "Best for Big Restaurants, Overkill for Others" ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/reviews/toast-pos)); its bar guide picks Toast for bars that serve food and Square for drink-only bars ([NerdWallet](https://nerdwallet.com/p/best/small-business/bar-pos-systems)); Owner.com's pizza roundup lists Toast as "best for restaurants offering more than just pizza" ([Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/blog/pizza-pos-system)). A Capterra pizza-chain reviewer who left Micros praised the reporting and complained that "online ordering and pre-ordering can't be used together" and that features are gated by tier ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=17)). Aggregate scores sit at **G2 4.2 of 5 and Capterra 4.2 of 5 with a 3.7 support sub-score** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/)).

The sales motion deserves as much study as the product. Toast's CRO has described dense territories staffed by local account executives who visit in person, with on-site visits closing at about **45 percent versus 15 percent without a visit** and one in five deals coming from referrals ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-strategies-toasts-cro-uses-to-crush-quotas)). The free Starter Kit lowers the decision to "try it." After signing, an Onboarding Consultant acts as "general contractor" through a four-to-six-week implementation covering kickoff, menu build, training, install and go-live, with a 14-day self-service path for online purchases ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Remote-Onsite-Onboarding-Guide)). A third-party dataset estimates about **1,800 Michigan restaurants on Toast, 94 percent of them independents**, led by Detroit, Grand Rapids and Ann Arbor, and Toast has posted a Detroit territory role ([Orbital](https://withorbital.com/data/tech/toast/michigan)). A Homer pizza tavern is squarely inside the territory model and has probably already had a visit.

### The complaints that recur from 2019 through 2026

Across Capterra, Trustpilot, the BBB and competitor compilations the same seven complaints repeat, and they have not changed in character since the oldest reviews. Capterra's automated summary finds **about 78 percent of 46 phone-support mentions negative and about 90 percent of 39 fee mentions negative**, centered on long holds, unreturned calls, hidden costs and billing corrections that drag ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/)). One owner's summary: "The monthly fees, hidden fees, and per-item charges add up to a very expensive bill" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=10)). Reviewers repeatedly contrast a responsive sales team with post-sale support that "sent requests to the wrong people and ignored follow-ups," including a bank-change request that took over two months ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=12)). Trustpilot US reviews cluster on unreturned calls, conflicting answers, deposit delays and held funds, and one roundup puts the score near 3.1 of 5 ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/toasttab.com); [CardPaymentOptions](https://www.cardpaymentoptions.com/point-of-sale/toast/)). BBB complaint counts are reported as 171 by one site and 235 by another and could not be verified ([PaymentPop](https://paymentpop.com/merchant-accounts/toast-pos-reviews/)).

The money complaints are structural. Toast Payments is mandatory on every tier, and the agreement reportedly lets Toast raise processing rates on 30 days' written notice, with continued use counting as acceptance ([Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/toast-pos-review/); [StartupOwl](https://startupowl.com/reviews/toast)). In September 2024 a William Blair note reported Toast "quietly raised its processing fees for its small and mid-sized restaurant clients by between .05% and .23%"; Toast said the increase applied to a limited number of clients and that it had not raised rates in the prior twelve years, and it rolled out compliant credit-card surcharging effective December 2024 ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/toast-processing-fee-increase-restaurants-surcharge-feature/729315/)). Contracts run two to three years, auto-renew for one-year periods unless written notice arrives 30 days before term end, and the early termination fee is essentially all remaining software fees for the term; a BBB complainant disputed a roughly **$1,600 ETF after a 24-month term auto-renewed** without, they said, clear flagging ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ma/boston/profile/point-of-sale-systems/toast-inc-0021-164973/complaints); [Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/toast-pos-review/)). Whether terminating after a rate increase waives the ETF is unresolved between sources: weAudit says it does, Merchant Insiders warns it can still apply ([weAudit](https://www.weaudit.com/ratings/toast/); [Merchant Insiders](https://merchantinsiders.com/blogs/toast-fees/)). Add-on stacking is the practical driver of the bill: POS USA reports "most single-location restaurants pay about $150 to $500/month for software and add-ons, before processing" ([POS USA](https://www.posusa.com/toast-pos-pricing/)), with online ordering around $75 a month, Toast Delivery Services at **$7.49 per order within five miles** ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Delivery-Services)) and payroll figures that conflict between $69 plus $9 to $10 per employee and $110 a month ([Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/toast-payroll-review/)). A competitor's full-stack worked example of $23,292 a year is that competitor's construction, not an operator invoice ([Sleft Payments](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/toast-pos-pricing-vs-independent-processing-2026)). Toast Capital deepens the lock: repayments come out of daily card sales before payout, one Trustpilot reviewer cites a 13 percent holdback, and an outstanding advance means the operator cannot leave ([Trustpilot CA](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/toasttab.com?page=2); [Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Capital-FAQ)).

Reliability complaints concentrate on one event. During the **October 20, 2025 AWS us-east-1 outage, which ran about 15 hours**, "many restaurants on the Toast point-of-sale system experienced complete system failures"; JD's Hamburgers in Fort Worth re-entered transactions by hand and a Cattleman's Roadhouse manager in Indiana offered to pay for meals because the restaurant could not run cards, while Square stayed largely up because it runs key products across multiple AWS regions ([Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-aws-outage-left-many-restaurants-scrambling); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/restaurant-tech-aws-outage-puts-spotlight-redundancy)). Toast's own documentation shows why offline mode did not fully cover that case: devices enter offline mode about 40 seconds after losing connectivity and keep taking orders and storing card payments for later authorization, but "offline devices communicate with each other through Toast's cloud services," checks cannot be closed, menu changes and 86ing are blocked, reports exclude offline activity until sync, kitchen routing survives only if UPnP multicast is enabled and a hardwired device acts as local hub, and "restaurants are responsible for offline transactions that are later declined, expire, or are disputed" ([Toast platform guide](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformOfflineMode.html); [Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Offline-Mode-Training-Guide); [Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformOfflineModeLocalSync.html)). Online orders placed during an outage do not reach devices until reconnection ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Outages-and-Disruptions-Toast-Online-Ordering)). No Toast post-incident report on the AWS outage was found.

Product-level weak spots are narrower. Toast's support site enumerates menu features that "will silently fail" when syncing to DoorDash, Uber Eats or Grubhub (time-specific pricing, pre-modifiers, subgroups, prompt-for-quantity, time-restricted menus) and warns nested modifiers must stay two layers deep ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Help-with-Third-Party-Ordering-Price-Incompatibilities); [Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Help-With-Menu-Issues-with-Third-Party-Delivery-DoorDash-Uber-Eats-Grubhub)); on Toast's own forum a merchant reported cancelled marketplace orders amounting to "hundred/ thousands of uncaptured sales" ([Toast Community](https://community.toasttab.com/t5/restaurant-operations/issues-with-doordash-amp-uber-integrations/m-p/16045)). Reporting complaints are about self-serve flexibility: order details cannot be exported to Excel for pivot tables, there is no report builder, and a Toast-hosted guide concedes custom reports and filters "cannot be saved or easily printed" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/); [Toast Tips PDF](http://d2c9w5yn32a2ju.cloudfront.net/knowledgebase/Toast-Tips-New-POS-Guide.pdf)). Security became an operator concern in 2025: unauthorized access to Toast's payroll application between June 30 and July 17 exposed names, SSNs and direct-deposit account numbers, with notification letters mailed from September 5 ([Strauss Borrelli](https://straussborrelli.com/2025/07/23/toast-data-breach-investigation/); [ClaimDepot](https://www.claimdepot.com/data-breach/toast-2025)), a competitor blog relays July 2025 Reddit reports of account takeovers that could not be verified ([Flyght](https://www.whatisflyght.com/blog/burnt-toast-when-your-pos-becomes-your-biggest-vulnerability)), and Toast's own help center confirms an active phishing campaign using lookalike domains ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Protect-Your-Toast-Account-From-Scammers)). A trade-secret suit by Gratuity Solutions survived a motion to dismiss in November 2025 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/toast-will-have-to-face-lawsuit-over-alleged-trade-secret-theft)). No merchant class action over fees was found.

### The nine-day life of the 99 cent fee

Because Glazed Web's own model rests on a 99 cent customer-paid order fee, the 2023 episode needs to be read closely. On June 24, 2023 the Boston Globe reported that Toast would add a **99 cent fee to online orders of $10 or more**, paid to Toast rather than the restaurant, visible only inside a combined "Taxes & Fees" line behind a small "i" icon, with no option for restaurants to cover it themselves; Toast said the fee "helps fund product investments" ([Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2023/06/24/new-toast-fee/)). It went nationwide on July 10. Within days Fox Business quoted owners calling it "medieval" and "unethical," and Koehn Bakery's Matt Wilhelmson said "I feel like [Toast] showed us that we can't trust them to be a business partner" ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/restaurateurs-fuming-ominous-new-fee-billed-customers-big-tech-vendor)); Rep. Mark Alford said the House Small Business Committee was "going full steam ahead with investigating the propriety of their way of doing business" ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/sudden-fees-charged-millions-americans-big-tech-vendor-toast-spark-congressional-query)). On July 19 CEO Chris Comparato announced removal: "We made the wrong decision and following a careful review, including the additional feedback we received, the fee will be removed from our Toast digital ordering channels," adding that Toast "would still adjust pricing thoughtfully" ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/update-on-toast-digital-ordering-for-our-customer-community); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-remove-99-cent-fee-after-widespread-backlash)). Shares fell between 12 and 16 percent on the day depending on the report ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/toast-inc-removes-hidden-fee-after-outrage-from-customers/456156)). Reforming Retail's headline the same day was that "Toast Merchants Will Ultimately Pay Up" ([Reforming Retail](https://reformingretail.com/index.php/2023/07/19/breaking-toast-to-remove-order-fee-by-end-of-week-but-toast-merchants-will-ultimately-pay-up/)), and the September 2024 processing increase and December 2024 surcharge feature bear that out. Fox Business later tied Comparato's replacement by Aman Narang, effective January 1, 2024, to the fee outrage; that causal framing is Fox's ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/big-tech-restaurant-vendor-toast-replace-ceo-fee-breach-ethical-leadership)). One review site claims a residual $0.49 fee for some merchants; that is unverified ([CardPaymentOptions](https://www.cardpaymentoptions.com/point-of-sale/toast/)). PYMNTS captured the objection precisely: Toast "is going around the restaurant and charging the restaurant's customer," and could do so only because it controls the POS ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/platform-payments/2023/will-99-cents-burn-toast-at-the-pos/)). The operators' grievance was consent, disclosure and the lack of an absorb option, not the dollar amount.

### Why operators choose Toast, why they stay, what makes them leave

Operators choose Toast because a local rep visited, neighboring restaurants run it, the entry price is zero and one vendor covers register, kitchen, online ordering, reservations, payroll, delivery dispatch and capital. They stay because each bundled module they adopt removes a second vendor, because the hardware runs only Toast, and because an ETF equal to the remaining term plus a possible Capital balance makes leaving expensive. They leave on events: a fee notice, a terminal that keeps failing, an outage during service, a surprise auto-renewal. The documented destinations are Square for no contract and no ETF, SpotOn for support (Tuscano's Italian Kitchen called support "a huge factor in making the switch," and SpotOn won G2's Best Support in summer 2024, per SpotOn's own release) and Clover on hardware cost, though Clover promotions carry a similar three-year term ([Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20240709489633/en/SpotOn-Recognized-for-Best-Support-Highest-User-Adoption-in-G2%e2%80%99s-Summer-2024-Grid-Report-for-Restaurant-Point-of-Sale/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/p/best/small-business/restaurant-pos-systems); [Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/blog/toast-vs-clover)). For a developer, the API mirrors the commercial model: a restaurant can self-provision read-only "Standard API access," which requires the Restaurant Management Suite Essentials tier per location, while any integration that creates orders needs the `orders.orders:write` scope issued only through an eight-stage partner program with unpublished fees; a single third-party help page says merchants pay $25 a month per location for partner-integration enablement, and that figure is single-source ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/devApiAccessUserGuide.html); [Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiOrdersFirstOrder.html); [Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/pos-api-access-and-what-integrations-cost); [Dolce](https://dolcesoftware.knowledgeowl.com/help/setting-up-toast-integration-with-dolce)).

## Aloha keeps ringing through outages and still loses independents

### Three products share one name

Anyone quoted "Aloha" in 2026 must ask which one. NCR Voyix sells **Aloha Essentials**, a subscription bundle around the Windows-based, on-premises Aloha POS that most existing customers run; **Aloha Cloud**, an Android product "well suited for independent restaurants and emerging chains with less complex operations" that is NCR Silver Pro renamed in 2022; and **Aloha Next by Voyix**, a cloud-to-edge microservices platform launched at the May 2026 National Restaurant Show for Chipotle first ([NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-essentials-pos); [NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud/about/release_notes/pos_release_notes/version-6x); [Fast Casual](https://www.fastcasual.com/news/chipotle-taps-ncr-voyix-to-launch-rollout-of-next-gen-pos/)). The corporate context matters because it shapes support. NCR split in October 2023 into NCR Voyix (restaurant and retail software) and NCR Atleos (ATMs) ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/business/2023/ncr-names-businesses-ahead-of-planned-separation/)); Voyix then sold digital banking for $2.45 billion, cut about 800 jobs in mid-2024 across sales, engineering and professional services, saw headcount fall from about 15,500 to about 13,500, installed former EVO Payments CEO James Kelly as CEO in February 2025 and handed hardware manufacturing to Ennoconn under an ODM deal, recognizing only commission on hardware from April 1, 2026 ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/ncr-voyix-layoffs/720575/); [NCR Voyix 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/70866/000007086626000006/ncr-20251231.htm); [SEC 8-K Feb 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/70866/000119312525020393/d817151d8k.htm); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/ncr-voyix-sells-digital-banking-business-to-veritas-capital/)). NCR's long-repeated figure is "more than 75,000 restaurants worldwide," a 2019 number; Voyix now reports about 78,000 platform sites company-wide, retail included, with no restaurant-only count ([NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/newsroom/resourced-globally-serving-locally-ncr-acquires-midwest-pos-growing-hospitality-presence-in-americas-heartland); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ncr-vyx-fiscal-q2-ebitda-jumps-20)). One 2026 review site attributes Aloha's support decline to "NCR/Shift4 ownership"; no Shift4 transaction with NCR Voyix exists and that attribution is wrong ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/reviews/aloha-reviews)).

### Why operators keep it

The retention story is reliability and depth rather than affection. A Capterra reviewer with more than two years on it said "the system handled high volume well," another called it "reliable" with support "available around the clock," and a bartender quoted in Bar & Restaurant said Aloha "is built for speed and good in crowded settings" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/178748/Aloha/reviews/); [Bar & Restaurant](https://www.barandrestaurant.com/operations/picking-pos)). The architecture is the reason. A franchisee IT knowledge base explains that "redundancy allows the Aloha Terminal to operate without the Aloha computer in the office (file server)," that terminals enter redundancy automatically, and that if the master terminal disappears the rest "reassign a new master terminal on their own after 15 to 45 seconds" ([Viva Chicken](https://vivachicken.happyfox.com/kb/article/20-aloha-troubleshooting/)). NCR markets Essentials as "all the benefits and functionality of the cloud with true redundancy onsite" ([NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-essentials-pos)). The table-service feature set is deep: tabs opened by card swipe with the card stored for later tendering, cards saved at seat level on one tab, transfers between tables, tabs and split checks, splits by seat, by equal payment or by item, per-function access levels, and fingerprint login ([NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/downloads/aloha-pos/TS_SavingDifferentCardstotheSameTabQRG-HKS1600.pdf); [NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud/using/working_with_tables_and_tabs)). Pizza is native: "Basic Pizza" charges a configurable percentage of the topping price for half toppings, and Deliverect's integration supports NCR's "Advanced Pizza" features ([NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/basic_pizza/pricing_pizzas_with_basic_pizza); [Deliverect](https://help.deliverect.com/en/articles/14107848-ncr-voyix-aloha-bsl-integration-overview)). Aloha Insight ships **more than 700 predefined reports** plus a drag-and-drop builder ([NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/insight)). Toast's own reviewers highlight what Aloha operators take for granted: several Capterra users found Toast's cash-drawer handling "confusing or inefficient" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=15)). The final reason to stay is cost of leaving: "migration from Aloha requires menu rebuilding and staff retraining," and even Toast-aligned Cali BBQ, which ran Aloha for twelve years, framed its switch as a digital-hospitality project with the POS swap as the means to it ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/reviews/aloha-vs-toast); [Cali BBQ](https://content.calibbq.media/switching-from-aloha-to-toast-pos/)).

### What they complain about

The complaint profile is the surround, not the terminal. Support leads: Capterra's NCR Aloha listing scores **3.7 of 5 on 83 reviews with customer service at 3.1 to 3.2**, with reviewers saying "most issues need to be escalated to a level 3 tech, and no one can be found when needed" and one "waiting over a year for help" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com.sg/reviews/190446/ncr-aloha-pos)). Aloha Cloud's Capterra page shows **69 percent of 80 service reviews and 84 percent of 50 technical-problem reviews negative** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/254668/Aloha-Cloud/reviews/)), G2 scores it 3.9 on about 335 reviews with "customer support response times can be slow" as the summary ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/aloha-cloud/reviews)), and a Trustpilot reviewer waited more than two weeks for a hardware technician ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ncrvoyix.com/restaurant)). Trustpilot's overall score for NCR Voyix restaurant is high, 4.4 on about 739 reviews, which conflicts with Capterra and G2 and suggests solicited post-install reviews ([rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/payments-fraud/point-of-sale-pos-systems/fiserv-clover/ncr-voyix-aloha-cloud)). Support quality is territorial, since dealers install and service; Michigan's "exclusive" Aloha dealer, F&B Management, is based in Scottsdale, Arizona, and no dealer with a Michigan office was identified ([F&B Management](https://www.fbmgmt.com/)).

Contracts and pricing come next. NCR publishes no Essentials price list. Its only official numbers are on the Aloha Cloud web store: a launch kit at $0 a month for hardware, **$145 a month per additional terminal kit, and processing at 2.95 percent plus 15 cents card-present and 3.95 percent plus 15 cents card-not-present** ([NCR Voyix](https://ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud-pos/buy/launch-kit); [NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud-pos/buy/order-confirmation)). A competitor's survey puts Essentials at $100 to $160 per terminal per month plus $2,500 to $10,000 implementation on a standard 36-month term ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/restaurant-pos-pricing-in-2026-real-costs-from-every-major-vendor)). NCR's own FAQ confirms "An NCR Payment Solutions service agreement is for 3 years" with a possible early termination fee ([NCR](https://www.docs.ncr.com/payments/general-payments-faq)). A Trustpilot reviewer wrote: "Once you try to cancel you find out and Aloha representative signed you up for a 3 year contract"; a Capterra Aloha Cloud reviewer said "they chain us to it" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ncrvoyix.com/restaurant); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/254668/Aloha-Cloud/reviews/)). Two figures are single-source and should be treated as claims: a Retail Systems post reports that in April 2025 NCR Voyix added 0.25 percent and up to 7 cents per authorization to existing customers' processing, about 48 basis points combined and roughly $1,800 a year for some restaurants ([Retail Systems](https://retailsystems.org/aloha-fees-increased/)), and two competitor pages say the Aloha Cloud merchant agreement allows annual increases of CPI plus 5 percent with a 5 percent floor ([RapidCents](https://rapidcents.com/en-us/resources/pos-comparisons/ncr-voyix-alternatives)). Whether NCR Payments is contractually mandatory for Essentials is disputed: review sites say it is required, while dealer Advanced Hospitality Systems says Essentials customers who choose NCR Payments get a subscription discount and can instead process with First Data ([POS USA](https://www.posusa.com/aloha-pos-review/); [Advanced Hospitality](https://advancedhospitality.com/ncr-voyix-aloha-essentials/)).

Operational complaints are about the on-premises model. NCR's own Table Service reference guide says store terminals "only receive the change through a refresh or the end-of-day process," and its docs warn that "All FOH terminals reboot during a refresh" and to "never [refresh] during peak hours of operation" ([NCR Voyix](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/downloads/aloha-pos/TS1911_ReferenceGuide-HKS1780.pdf); [NCR Voyix](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/digital-ordering/implementing/refreshing_pos_data)). A price correction during dinner either waits for close or reboots every register, which cloud systems push in seconds. Online ordering is the weakest surface. Every digital order, NCR's own or a marketplace's, lands through Aloha Takeout and the Business Services Layer, which require Aloha POS 12.3 or 15.1 and ATO 19.x or later ([NCR Voyix](https://www.ncrvoyix.com/about/vendor-integration); [NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-takeout/integrating/integrating_ato_and_bsl/integrating_ato_and_bsl)); Olo's prerequisites state that "if running more than one External Interface, you are required by NCR to obtain a license for each," so each ordering channel carries its own license and a dealer install ([Olo](https://olosupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000632923-Aloha-POS-Prerequisites-and-Olo-Service-Installation)); Deliverect's standard fix for recurring validation errors is "restart your POS and ATO" ([Deliverect](https://help.deliverect.com/en/articles/8499544-ncr-aloha-resolve-issues)); and DirectOrders describes the platform as "not originally built around a direct-to-consumer online ordering channel" ([DirectOrders](https://www.directorders.com/integrations/ncr-aloha)). In April 2023 a BlackCat ransomware attack "shut down Aloha POS apps that power online ordering" and back-office functions while in-store transactions continued; a franchise operator quoted via The Stack was "doing the old pen and paper right now and sending to head office," and NCR later said it "built a new cloud environment" for the affected applications ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/ncrs-aloha-pos-hit-cyberattack-causing-headaches-restaurants); [The Stack](https://www.thestack.technology/aloha-pos-outage-ransomware-recovery/); [SEC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/70866/000007086623000044/ncr-20230930.htm)). Data ownership is thin on both lines: Aloha Cloud's back office shows only "a rolling 13 months" of transactions and exports two CSV files ([NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-cloud/results/viewing_pos_transactions)), while legacy reporting is split across Aloha Manager on the store server, hosted back office, Aloha Kitchen and the Pulse app, which carries a **1.8 of 5 App Store rating on 473 reviews** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id477555106); [Avero](https://averoinc.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500003148562-Aloha-POS)). The API is partner-gated with roughly 28 cloud REST APIs and no self-serve signup; a claim that partners pay 30 to 50 percent of ISV revenue comes from a vendor selling its own unofficial layer and is unverified ([Supergood](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/aloha-ncr)).

### Voyix's own numbers confirm the drain

NCR Voyix's calls document the independent exodus without naming competitors. Q4 2025 cited "weaker SMB performance" against recurring enterprise and mid-market growth of 6 percent ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/26/ncr-voyix-vyx-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). Q1 2026 restaurant revenue was $179 million, down 6 percent, with "continued softness in the SMB restaurant segment, which remains a headwind until the new 'restaurant-in-a-box' solution launches" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ncr-voyix-corporation-q1-2026-123000434.html)). Q2 2026 restaurant revenue fell 6 percent excluding the hardware transition, **platform sites rose 12 percent while payment sites fell 1 percent**, customers "delayed refreshes, likely into next year," and the SMB "Store-in-a-Box" was slated for customer labs by the end of Q3 2026 and pilots in Q1 2027 ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/ncr-voyix-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-09/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ncr-voyix-q2-earnings-call-150420374.html)). Growth is enterprise seats on Aloha Next (Chipotle's roughly 4,000 units under a six-year exclusive, Sbarro's nearly 300 and Pizza Ranch's 200-plus) while payment-attached SMB sites shrink ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/918488/ncr-voyix-to-deploy-aloha-next-across-nearly-300-sbarro-locations-in-the-u-s/)). Marco's Pizza still chose Essentials in October 2025 for international expansion, which shows the on-premises product is being held, with no retirement announced ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251020351372/en/marco%e2%80%99s-pizza-selects-ncr-voyix-to-power-international-expansion/)). Toast's CEO said on the Q4 2025 call that "we're seeing customers switch from legacy on-prem solutions, similar to what we saw in restaurants 10 years ago" ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/12/toast-tost-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). A deferred hardware refresh is the moment an independent on a proprietary platform typically leaves, and Voyix just reported a wave of deferred refreshes.

The pattern is that Toast fails upward (cloud-dependent, so a regional AWS outage or a lost WAN removes features) while Aloha fails outward (the register survives anything, and everything around it, from menu refresh to online orders to support escalation, is slow or gated). Both monetize the same way, through bundled payments and term contracts, and both are complained about for exactly that.

## Across the market, the processing spread outweighs the software fee

### Generalists and the pizza specialists

List prices for entry software cluster low and recover margin elsewhere. Square for Restaurants is free, $49 Plus or $149 Premium with in-person processing at 2.6 percent plus 15 cents and "no long-term contracts or termination fees," though 24/7 support is only on Premium and tier-based rates of 2.5 and 2.4 percent are reported by one source ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/best/small-business/restaurant-pos-systems); [Chowly](https://chowly.com/resources/blogs/toast-vs-square-for-restaurants/)). Clover direct lists a Table Service plan at $89.95 a month and 2.3 percent plus 10 cents, but Clover reaches most small merchants through banks and ISOs that set their own fees, so "the same Clover Station commonly runs $1,200 or $1,800+" and reseller bundles of $135 to $245 a month on 36-month terms are common ([nextdoorpayments](https://nextdoorpayments.com/clover/for-restaurants); [AZ Restaurant Partners](https://azrestaurantpartners.com/toast-vs-clover.html)). SpotOn's rates are reported as a free tier at 2.79 percent plus 20 cents, Essentials at $55 per station at 2.45 percent plus 15 cents, or 1.99 percent plus 25 cents on paid plans, figures that conflict between sources ([tech.co](https://tech.co/pos-system/best-restaurant-pos-systems); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/best/small-business/restaurant-pos-systems)); SpotOn topped G2's Fall 2025 restaurant POS grid with a score of 89 against Toast's 76, per SpotOn's release ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/private-companies/spoton-tops-g2s-fall-2025-pos-rankings-surpassing-toast)). SkyTab (Shift4) is about $29.99 per terminal with "free" hardware on a 36-month commitment, 2.75 percent plus 15 cents list, and a reported $250 annual program fee per device that one critic calls an equipment lease in disguise ([Merchant Cost Consulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/skytab-pos-review/); [SkyTab Partners](https://www.skytabpartners.us/blog/skytab-pricing-fees-hidden-costs/)). TouchBistro is $69 or $119, Lightspeed Restaurant $69, $189 or $399, Revel $99 per terminal on a three-year contract with a two-terminal minimum ([tech.co](https://tech.co/pos-system/best-restaurant-pos-systems); [POS USA](https://www.posusa.com/revel-systems-pos-review/)).

The pizza specialists charge two to three times the generalists' software price and sell on delivery and modifier depth. Thrive lists Delivery Plus at $149 and The Works at $199 a month and requires ThrivePay processing on every plan ([Thrive](https://www.thrivepos.com/pricing); [POS USA](https://www.posusa.com/thrive-pos-review/)); HungerRush is quote-based with reported starting points of $99 or $199 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/122189/HungerRush360/)); SpeedLine is quote-only with a single Capterra listing at CA$4,300 as a one-time starting price ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.ie/compare/30009/122189/speedline-pizza-pos/vs/hungerrush360)). Their wins are Michigan-relevant: SpeedLine took Happy's Pizza's 65-plus Detroit-area stores on reliability and Cottage Inn's 56 locations as exclusive partner, and Jet's Pizza of Sterling Heights chose Revention (now HungerRush) ([Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/happys-pizza-taps-speedline-solutions-65-store-pos-install); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/cottage-inn-pizza-taps-speedline-pos/); [Verdict Foodservice](https://www.verdictfoodservice.com/news/jets-pizza-selects-revention-pos-solutions)). Every specialist cited won regional chains first and no case of a local dealer or regional software shop beating Toast for independents was found. SpeedLine's feature set is the pizza benchmark: deferred orders up to 30 days ahead, a configurable window in which deferred orders are refused "to prevent kitchen overload," promise-time quotes that update with kitchen volume, and caller ID that greets by name and repeats the last order ([SpeedLine](https://www.speedlinesolutions.com/blog/speedline-product-roundup-winter-2021); [SpeedLine](https://www.speedlinesolutions.com/hubfs/Portals/0/PublicWebsiteDocs_SF/MarketingDocs_SF/Ebook_Delivery_Management.pdf)).

A worked example from the market note makes the economics concrete. The researcher assumed a single-location pizza and bar doing $80,000 a month in in-person card volume across 2,300 transactions on two terminals, and computed software plus rate plus per-item fees from the list rates above; these are estimates, not quotes.

| System (list rates) | Monthly in-person cost | Effective rate incl. software |
|---|---|---|
| Clover direct, $89.95 at 2.3% + 10¢ | about $2,160 | 2.70% |
| SpotOn, 2 stations at 1.99% + 25¢ | about $2,277 | 2.85% |
| Toast $69 at 2.49% + 15¢ | about $2,406 | 3.01% |
| SpotOn, 2 stations at 2.45% + 15¢ | about $2,415 | 3.02% |
| Square Plus $49 at 2.6% + 15¢ | about $2,474 | 3.09% |
| SkyTab, 2 terminals, 2.75% + 15¢ | about $2,647 | 3.31% |
| Toast pay-as-you-go at 3.09% + 15¢ | about $2,817 | 3.52% |
| Interchange-plus benchmark, processing only | about $1,600-$1,680 | 2.0-2.1% before software |

The subscription is a rounding error next to the spread: a half-point difference in processing on $80,000 is $400 a month, more than the whole gap between Square's free tier and Lightspeed Essentials. Visa's published consumer-credit interchange runs about 1.51 to 2.30 percent plus 10 cents card-present, modeled interchange-plus effective rates sit near 2.0 to 2.1 percent in person and 2.5 percent online against 2.7 to 2.95 percent and 3.2 percent on flat-rate plans, and the gap narrows or vanishes when the card mix is heavy in rewards and commercial cards ([Celer Merchants](https://celermerchants.com/guides/flat-rate-vs-interchange-plus/); [ProTech Payments](https://protechpayments.com/interchange-plus-vs-flat-rate-pricing/); [Merchant Cost Consulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/interchange-plus-savings/)). Toast's 48 to 49 basis point net payments take, which management attributes to "cost optimization, small pricing moves, and new products such as surcharging," is the yardstick: on roughly $1 million of annual card volume that is about $5,000 a year from one pizzeria against $828 from a $69 subscription ([Toast Q3 2025 prepared remarks](https://s28.q4cdn.com/141746709/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Q3-25-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). Katalyst's break-even arithmetic says Toast's $0 plan costs more than the $69 plan once card sales pass about $11,500 a month ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/toast-free-plan-break-even-for-new-restaurants)).

### First-party ordering layers and the marketplaces they replace

The first-party ordering products land at 3.5 to 6 percent of order value against 15 to 30 percent on marketplaces. Square Online charges no monthly fee and 2.9 percent plus 30 cents ([ChowNow](https://get.chownow.com/blog/alternatives-to-toast-online-ordering/)); ChowNow is reported at $119, $229 or $328 a month with 2.95 percent plus 29 cents processing ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/chownow-vs-popmenu)); Popmenu runs $179 to $499 with undisclosed processing ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-vs-popmenu)); BentoBox charges **$49 a month plus 99 cents per order plus 3 percent**, which its own worked example puts at about 6 percent of gross on 200 orders a month ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/bentobox-pricing-fees)); Owner.com charges the restaurant $249 a month plus 5 percent, or $499 flat, and on both plans **guests pay a 5 percent "order support fee" at checkout** ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-competitors); [restauranttools.ai](https://restauranttools.ai/tools/owner-com)). GloriaFood, the free option many small shops used, is reported by a competitor to have closed to new sign-ups in 2025 with a shutdown set for April 30, 2027; that is unconfirmed against Oracle ([storekit](https://gloria-food.storekit.com/blog/gloriafood-closed-to-new-restaurants)). Slice built a pizza-only network on a fixed **$2.25 per order plus 4 percent processing** as of 2020, waived on orders of $10 or less, and later bundled a register, payments and driver management into tiered per-order packages; its 2025 to 2026 schedule could not be reconciled from the excerpts ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201028005373/en); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/slice-raises-43-million-to-help-pizzerias-go-online); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/slice-launches-tiered-service-packages-for-independent-pizzerias/605158/)). Marketplaces charge DoorDash 15, 25 or 30 percent on delivery and 6 percent on pickup, with Uber Eats on a similar ladder and a "commission-free" webshop at 2.5 percent plus 29 cents ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/doordash-fees-for-restaurants-2); [getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/uber-eats-fees-for-restaurants-2)). On the researcher's 500-orders-at-$40 example, Square Online costs about $730 (3.65 percent), ChowNow Pro about $964 (4.8 percent), BentoBox about $1,144 (5.7 percent), Owner.com Flexible about $1,249 to the restaurant plus $1,000 charged to guests, and marketplace delivery $3,000 to $6,000. Glazed Web's flat 99 cents on that order mix is $495 a month, or 2.5 percent, paid by guests, and it sits below every product on the list while matching BentoBox's per-order unit and undercutting Owner.com's guest-side percentage by half.

### Who can bring a processor and who can use an API

Processor choice is rare. Toast, Square, SkyTab and Thrive tie software to their own payments, and Clover's processing depends on whichever bank or ISO sold it; a competing processor lists SpotOn, Revel, Lightspeed and TouchBistro as accepting outside processors, but that is an interested party's claim and conflicts with reports that those vendors default new US accounts to their own payments ([Sleft Payments](https://www.sleftpayments.com/learning-hub/why-restaurants-leaving-toast-pos-2026)). Developer access splits the same way. Square and Clover offer self-serve sandboxes and public docs, with Clover reviewing production accounts and App Market listings ([Clover](https://docs.clover.com/docs/get-started-with-sandbox-environment); [Clover](https://docs.clover.com/docs/clover-app-approval-process); [Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/reviews/toast-vs-square)). Toast issues write credentials only through its partner program, with a "restaurant management group" account type available to a developer working on one owner's locations ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiClientAccounts.html)). SpotOn keys "are not self-provisioned" and require a sales rep or customer success manager ([SpotOn](https://developers.spoton.com/enterprise/docs/enabling-api-access)). Olo, which serves 750-plus brands and about 88,000 locations and was taken private by Thoma Bravo in 2025, runs a tiered, login-gated partner program with no self-serve sandbox and is irrelevant to a single pizzeria ([Olo](https://partners.olo.com/downloads/olo-connect-partner-program-guide.pdf); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/mergers-acquisitions/olo-to-be-acquired-by-software-investment-firm-thoma-bravo)). A small studio can therefore build and test against Square and Clover today, can build for one Toast owner's locations through the group account, and cannot sell an order-injecting product to Toast or Aloha restaurants without months of partner certification.

### How independents decide and how long they stay

Stated switching intent is high and actual tenure is long. A Hospitality Technology study relayed secondhand says **44 percent of operators plan to replace or significantly upgrade their POS in 2026** ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/the-great-pos-migration)); TouchBistro's survey found 53 percent bought or switched in the past year, down from 71 percent, with features (60 percent) and lower fees (57 percent) the top reasons among switchers and reliability, ease of use and price the top evaluation criteria ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/%ef%bf%bceye-on-restaurant-pos-full-service-eateries-rebound-as-they-adopt-new-payments-tech/)). Those figures include first-time buys and add-ons and should not be read as churn. Inertia is explained by time: a Toast-commissioned study of 652 US SMB decision makers found 37 percent opt out of switching "simply because it would take time" ([Restaurant Dive, sponsored](https://www.restaurantdive.com/spons/choosing-a-pos-system-what-restaurant-operators-prioritize-in-2026/815164/)), and consultants put single-location migration at **$3,000 to $6,000** with internal labor "often 2-3x the vendor implementation fees and almost never tracked," three to six weeks from contract to cutover, and ETFs anywhere from nominal to $10,000-plus ([Tabres](https://tabres.com/articles/restaurant-pos-system-cost); [Tableview](https://www.tableview.com/blog/switching-restaurant-pos-systems/); [Snappy](https://gosnappy.io/blog/cost-of-switching-pos-systems-for-restaurants/)). Channel shapes the choice: "Toast mainly sells direct, while Clover reaches smaller merchants through banks and resellers," and Square is self-serve ([Payments in Full](https://paymentsinfull.substack.com/p/clover-vs-square-vs-toast)). TouchBistro's 2026 report found 66 percent of independents carry debt and margins back to double digits for the first time since 2022, which is the financial mood a 2026 pitch lands in ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127769924/en/U.S.-Restaurants-Overcome-Financial-Strain-TouchBistros-2026-American-State-of-Restaurants-Report-Reveals-Double-Digit-Profit-Margins-and-Tech-Driven-Resilience)). For a Michigan independent the realistic buying paths are a Toast territory rep, the bank pushing Clover, a pizza-specialist dealer, or self-serve Square; a new entrant with no field channel competes in the self-serve lane where Square already sits, and the only way around that is the referral and peer-proof motion Toast itself relies on.

## A Homer pizza tavern needs pre-auth tabs, fractional toppings and Michigan's tax rules

### Day-one register features

The target in the repository is Cascarelli's of Homer, a ninety-year-old pizza restaurant with 20 drafts (19 from Michigan), a packaged-nut shop already on Stripe Checkout, draft beer and wine to go, live piano and a banquet room. The day-one register for that profile, ranked from the vendor evidence, is: a menu with size, crust and fractional toppings and a configurable half-topping rule, since Aloha charges a percentage, NextOrder and SalesVu offer "price of the more expensive half, average the two halves, or per topping per half," and PMQ's operator forum shows shops split on the policy ([NCR Voyix docs](https://docs.ncrvoyix.com/restaurant/aloha-pos/implementing/basic_pizza/pricing_pizzas_with_basic_pizza); [SalesVu](https://static.salesvu.com/industries/pizza-shops/); [PMQ](https://thinktank.pmq.com/t/half-orders/10087)); dine-in, carryout and phone order types with open checks and seat numbers; bar tabs opened by one card tap with pre-authorization, which Square's community has demanded across a 62-page thread titled "Beating a Dead Horse but it needs to be: Preauthorization" while bartenders "keep the card in a holder or the register until the customer is ready to tab out" ([Square Seller Community](https://sellercommunity.com/t5/Square-Point-of-Sale-app/Beating-a-Dead-Horse-but-it-needs-to-be-Preauthorization/idi-p/354899/page/62); [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Orders-Menu-Items-Catalog/CC-Pre-Authorization-for-bar-tabs/m-p/748448/highlight/true)); a flat draft grid with pint, pitcher and growler-fill as one-tap variants instead of a modifier dialog, because Capterra Toast reviewers specifically wished for bartender "speed screens" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136301/Toast-POS/reviews/?page=5)); splits by seat and by item and transfers between tab and table; station routing to the oven, grill and bar on printers or KDS; 86ing that propagates to online ordering; discounts, comps and voids behind a manager PIN with reason codes; cash drawer kick, blind counts and an end-of-day report that separates sales tax, tips, cash and card; tip entry on card receipts and tip declaration at clock-out; and promise-time quoting with order-ahead and a "no deferred orders during the rush" window on SpeedLine's pattern. Coursing and fire times are full-service features; a pizza tavern needs "fire now or hold" per ticket. Delivery dispatch, loyalty, gift cards, house accounts, inventory, menu-mix reports, scheduling, kiosks and handhelds can follow.

### Michigan rules the register must encode

Michigan's rules land in four places. Sales tax: the rate is 6 percent with no local add-ons; "food and food ingredients" are exempt and "prepared food" is taxable, defined as food sold heated, two or more ingredients combined by the seller, or sold with utensils provided by the seller; Public Acts 141 and 142 of 2023, effective February 13, 2024, added a special standard under which a seller whose prepared-food sales exceed 75 percent is treated as providing utensils if they are merely "made available" ([Michigan Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/treasury/reference/taxpayer-notices/2024/01/25/changes-to-the-tax-treatment-of-prepared-food); [RAB 2024-13](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/rep-legal/rab/2024-revenue-administrative-bulletins/revenue-administrative-bulletin-2024-13)). A newer bulletin, **RAB 2026-2 issued January 29, 2026**, replaces RAB 2024-13 and could not be read ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/michigan-treasury-issues-new-guidance-on-sales-tax-for-prepared-food-items)). Food that is only cut or repackaged is not prepared food ([RAB 2009-8](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/rep-legal/rab/2009-revenue-administrative-bulletins/revenue-administrative-bulletin-2009-8)), but TaxJar says prepackaged items sold by carry-out restaurants are taxable while tax-rates.org says candy and soda are exempt groceries, and neither is primary ([TaxJar](https://www.taxjar.com/blog/food/is-food-taxable-in-michigan); [tax-rates.org](https://www.tax-rates.org/michigan/sales-tax)). The practical read for Cascarelli's, whose README leaves Stripe Tax off "until their accountant says which nuts are taxable," is that a pizza tavern almost certainly exceeds 75 percent prepared-food sales, so nuts sold at the bar default to taxable under the "made available" standard while mail-order nuts shipped without utensils are the stronger exemption case; the register needs tax categories per item and per channel, and the accountant needs RAB 2026-2.

Alcohol to go: the MLCC FAQ says a qualified licensee may sell beer, wine, spirits or cocktails for off-premises consumption only in a "qualified container," meaning clean, sealable, one gallon or less, sealed by the licensee after filling, with no straw holes and not filled before the sale; an on-premises licensee "must not sell alcoholic liquor to a customer to go in its original container" unless it also holds an SDM license, in which case original-container beer and wine are allowed; delivered containers must carry a "Contains Alcohol" label and the recipient must show ID ([MLCC](https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/lcc/faq/alcohol-to-go)). The enabling statute, MCL 436.1537a, was written with a **December 31, 2025 sunset**, a 2023 bill (SB 141, listed as PA 95 of 2023) was introduced to remove it, and the research could not confirm whether the authority is still in force ([MRLA](https://www.mrla.org/august23fsbr.html); [MichiganVotes](https://michiganvotes.org/legislation/2023/senate/bill-141)). The register therefore needs a to-go alcohol item class that forces a sealed-container flag and size, blocks original-container sales unless the licensee's SDM flag is set, prompts an ID check and is switchable per licensee.

Labor: Michigan's minimum wage is **$13.73 from January 1, 2026, with a tipped cash wage of $5.49 (40 percent) and a maximum tip credit of $8.24**, rising to $15.00 on January 1, 2027 and then indexed, with the tipped percentage climbing two points a year toward 50 percent by 2031 under the February 2025 legislative deal that replaced the court-ordered schedule ([HiveDesk](https://www.hivedesk.com/compliance/united-states/minimum-wage-michigan); [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-government/michigans-minimum-wage-is-going-up-in-2026-heres-why/); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/michigan-accelerates-minimum-wage-increases-saves-tip-credit)). The labor report needs a per-shift tip-credit makeup check so cash wage plus tips reach $13.73 ([TipHaus](https://www.tiphaus.com/blog/michigan-tipping-laws-explained-minimum-wage-pooling-and-your-rights/)). Federal tip rules add the inputs for IRS Form 8027, which applies to food-and-beverage establishments with more than ten employees on a typical day and requires allocating tips when reported tips fall below 8 percent of gross receipts; the register must emit gross receipts, charged receipts, charged tips, reported cash tips and hours by employee ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc761); [Fit Small Business](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/form-8027-instructions/)). Happy-hour pricing: secondary sources say Michigan bars may not discount a drink more than 50 percent off regular price, sell below cost, run unlimited-drink pricing or restrict a promotion to some customers, and that two-for-one is allowed within those limits; the MLCC rule text was not retrieved, so the pricing engine should treat the 50 percent rule as a configurable guardrail rather than hard law ([State Regs Today](https://www.stateregstoday.com/?p=15192)).

## One developer can ship the register; offline and payments are the long poles

### Square Terminal or Stripe Terminal, and the platform fee

Both processors the studio already codes against can carry a platform fee, and neither lets the studio take that fee from its own merchant account. Square Terminal is **$299**, works only with Square processing, and a `CreateTerminalCheckout` can carry `app_fee_money` deposited to the developer's account, but Square staff have said "Terminal API will not support collecting app_fee_money from the same Square account the application is associated to, and there are no plans to support this due to compliance reasons," so the restaurant must be a separate Square account OAuth'd to the studio's app with the `PAYMENTS_WRITE_ADDITIONAL_RECIPIENTS` scope ([Square](https://square.com/help/us/en/article/6538-square-terminal-faq); [Square](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/take-payments-and-collect-fees); [Square forum](https://developer.squareup.com/forums/t/terminal-and-app-fee/4839)). The Terminal API "does not support offline mode" and Square's advice is a phone hotspot, although the Square POS app itself stores offline payments for up to 72 hours on Register, Terminal and Handheld ([Square forum](https://developer.squareup.com/forums/t/how-square-terminal-work-in-offline-mode/13703); [Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8106)). Stripe Terminal readers are the M2 at **$59** (Bluetooth, needs a native SDK) and the S700 and S710 at **$299**, with the WisePOS E unavailable to new orders after September 1, 2026; US in-person processing is 2.7 percent plus 5 cents, `application_fee_amount` on a PaymentIntent made on behalf of a connected account carries the fee, and the S700 and S710 support an offline mode whose limits were not found ([payment.co](https://blog.payment.co/which-stripe-card-reader-should-you-buy/); [Quilt](https://quilt-payfac.notion.site/WisePOS-E-Card-Reader-Discontinued-d74b6d2366224198b36ae5653d4d7000); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/terminal); [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/marketplace/tasks/app-fees); [BizSwoop](https://www.bizswoop.com/hub/stripe-terminal-hardware-2026-reader-guide/)). Adyen's reported $120 monthly minimum rules it out at this scale ([Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/adyen-review/)). Offline card capture is the deciding difference: Stripe's S700 or S710 if it matters, and if the studio stays on Square the honest answer is "cards need internet; keep a hotspot."

### Offline design and the local hub question

Toast's production design is the benchmark: an eligible hardwired, non-handheld device is auto-selected as "the communication point between all Toast devices on a local network," relaying orders to printers and KDS by UPnP multicast on a single subnet while the cloud is unreachable, and offline card payments should be reconnected within three days, preferably 24 hours ([Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformOfflineModeLocalSync.html); [Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminOfflineCCPayments.html)). A Vercel-hosted Next.js register has three independent failure domains (the restaurant's ISP, Vercel and its database, and the processor) and no local component, so it fails closed on all three unless the UI is a PWA that boots from cache, the menu and open checks are mirrored in IndexedDB, writes go through an outbox with idempotency keys replayed on reconnect, and printing has a LAN path; an Odoo add-on documents the same pattern of queued orders, a live connection indicator and replay with conflicts surfaced for review ([ECOSIRE](https://ecosire.com/apps/odoo/pos-offline-mode-pro)). The studio's existing CloudPRNT printer integration does not survive an ISP outage because CloudPRNT polls the cloud by design; Star's CloudPRNT utility does support a `[drawer]` markup tag and `DrawerOpenTime` of StartOfJob or EndOfJob, so a drawer-only job is the likely cloud "no sale," with lag equal to the poll interval ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/StarMicronics.CloudPRNT-Utility); [Star Micronics](https://star-m.jp/products/s_print/sdk/StarCloudPRNT/manual/en/dotnet-api-reference/starmicronics-cloudprnt/drawer-open-time.html)). The options in rising cost are a PWA with outbox and hotspot for cards; a small always-on LAN box running the same app with a database replica that acts as Toast's hub and a local print server, which is the closest analogue to what Toast ships; or a native tablet app with an embedded database on Floreant's model, which also unlocks the $59 M2 reader and Tap to Pay ([Linux.com](https://www.linux.com/?p=474992)). PCI scope stays manageable: with a PTS-listed semi-integrated reader where card data never touches the app, the merchant is likely eligible for SAQ B-IP, and the studio as a software vendor that never handles a PAN stays out of card-present scope, though no PCI SSC listing validating Stripe or Square readers as P2PE was found ([Surfboard Payments](https://support.surfboardpayments.com/pci-dss-self-assessment-questionnaire-saq-for-in-store-payments); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/point-to-point-encryption-p2pe)).

### Effort and price

No source gives a person-month figure for a minimum register, and the open-source histories warn that Floreant and uniCenta took years to reach feature-complete even as Java desktop apps without payments. The researcher's estimate, given an existing online-ordering, kitchen, CloudPRNT and Stripe and Square codebase, is that the net-new surface (touch order entry with fractional modifiers and a drafts grid, tabs with pre-auth, split and transfer, payments orchestration with tips, cash, partial payments and PIN-gated refunds, cash management and the Z report, a Michigan tax engine, employee PIN and clock, the offline shell, and exports) is **four to six calendar months for a version one friendly restaurant can run on two terminals**, with the offline layer and payments as the long poles, and another three to six months before it is safe to sell to a second restaurant with multi-tenant onboarding, per-licensee tax and alcohol configuration, support tooling and hardware provisioning. Pricing must sit where the market sits: a base of $0 to $99 a month per location, margin from a 0.2 to 0.5 percent application fee riding on 2.6 to 2.7 percent processing plus per-device add-ons, and Toast's own all-in estimate for a $2 million full-service restaurant of $1,100 to $1,800 a month as the ceiling the pitch is measured against ([Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/restaurant-pos-pricing-in-2026-real-costs-from-every-major-vendor); [Upmenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/toast-pricing)). Two-terminal hardware is about $600 in readers on either processor plus a tablet or PC, Star printer and drawer.

## Glazed Web's 99 cent fee survives with consent, itemization and an opt-out

The successes to recreate are concrete. Toast's handheld-plus-KDS loop and one-tap pre-auth tabs are what full-service and bar operators cite when they pick it over Square; the studio already owns the kitchen side and should make pre-auth tabs and the draft grid the register's first screens. Toast's "general contractor" onboarding, a structured checklist with a menu import and a named person through go-live, is cheap to replicate and is the step reviewers remember. Toast's field motion is the real engine behind 30,000 net adds a year; a one-person studio cannot staff territories, and referrals from a visible neighboring restaurant, which are one in five of Toast's deals, are the only channel a Homer launch can use. Aloha's lesson is that the register must keep ringing and tendering when everything else is dead, and that operators will tolerate a dated interface for decades if that holds. Toast Tables' $50 reservations tier shows a bundled module priced far under the incumbent is a wedge, and Cascarelli's banquet room and live piano already run on a party form.

The failures to patch are the documented complaints, and each maps to a product decision. Publish a one-page price and exit policy: month-to-month after year one, no ETF, hardware the operator owns, full export in open formats, and no rate changes without a signed amendment; this inverts the auto-renewal, 30-day notice and unilateral-rate clauses that generate the bulk of Toast and Aloha grievances. Price processing as interchange-plus with a stated markup so the single largest line item is visible, which no bundled platform does. Keep marketplace menu publishing as a validated, logged pipeline with pre-flight checks and a change log, since Toast's silent sync failures and missing menu log are its clearest product-level fault. Ship saved custom reports and raw CSV export of order lines on day one, since both incumbents fail there. Make menu and price edits propagate in seconds without a reboot, which is the single thing Aloha operators feel most. Design support around the 7 pm Saturday scenario with a live human inside five minutes during service hours and remote diagnostics, because support is the one complaint that has not changed across seven years of reviews on either platform. Require MFA and out-of-band verification for credential changes, because 2025 brought a payroll breach and phishing to Toast's base.

The fee model needs the most care, because the studio's number is Toast's number. Toast failed on consent (no opt-out), on disclosure (buried under "Taxes & Fees" behind an icon) and on who got paid (Toast, invisibly). The market has since normalized operator-chosen guest fees: Owner.com charges guests 5 percent on every order and BentoBox charges restaurants 99 cents per order, and both survive. A 99 cent fee on a $40 pizza order is 2.5 percent, below Square Online, ChowNow, BentoBox and Owner.com, and far below any marketplace. It is viable on four conditions drawn from the 2023 record: the operator enables it and can switch to absorbing it; the guest sees it on its own labeled line, never inside a tax total; the fee is waived or capped on small orders the way Slice waives its $2.25 under $10; and the policy is in the signed agreement so it cannot be changed unilaterally. The research note on market demand flagged Owner.com's guest-side 5 percent as a latent vulnerability for the same reason, and a Michigan independent whose guests blame the restaurant for a fee it did not choose will churn exactly as Toast's did.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the competitive question. Toast and Aloha are both bundled-payments businesses wearing software, and every durable complaint about them, from the 2023 fee to the 2024 and 2025 rate increases to the 36-month terms, is a consequence of needing 48 to 98 basis points of card volume to pay for free hardware and $0 software. That means a challenger cannot match the entry price and should not try; it should sell the inverse, a visible processing markup, an exit policy and hardware that stays, to the operators the incumbents' own numbers show are leaving: Voyix's SMB sites and the Toast customers whose renewals, failed terminals or 15-hour cloud outages are the documented switch triggers. The second shift is that offline resilience, long treated as Aloha's legacy quirk, is now the live differentiator after October 2025, and a web register on Vercel inherits Toast's weakness unless it adds a local hub, which is the one piece of hardware the studio should be willing to ship.

What remains unknown shapes the first months of work. Nothing here verifies a live Toast rate card, the current Merchant Agreement, NCR's Essentials pricing or Michigan's alcohol-to-go status after December 31, 2025, and no first-hand operator thread was read; the first three conversations with Cascarelli's and its accountant, plus a read of RAB 2026-2 and MCL 436.1537a as amended, will settle more than another round of searching. The studio's existing assets, an ordering layer, a kitchen screen, printer code and two processors, cover roughly half of a day-one register; the half that is missing, tabs with pre-auth, cash control, the Michigan tax and tip engine, and an outbox that survives a dead ISP, is also the half that no incumbent does well for a small pizza tavern, which is the opening.
